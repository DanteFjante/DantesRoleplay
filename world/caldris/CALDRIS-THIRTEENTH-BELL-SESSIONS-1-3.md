This packet prepares situations, people, clues, and consequences. It does not decide what the
characters do, what checks are called for, or which explanation they accept. Any sensible plan
that respects the physical constraints should move the mystery forward.

## The mystery in one page

At noon in heavy rain, Bramblebridge's civic bell rings thirteen times. An apparently driverless
tax wagon enters the market, its canvas wet and its flour strangely dry. The event looks like a
haunting, prank, or warning. It is a coordinated six-minute diversion: accomplices used theatre
rigging to imitate and then move the bell while selected cargo changed hands in a lane omitted from
the new watch map.

The missing driver, **Edra Voss**, is alive. She abandoned the seat under coercion after being told
her brother's ferry debt would be called in. She then doubled back to protect the horse, **Turnip**,
and became trapped in an old lime shed when a swollen drain shifted the door. The visible organizer
is **Fara Dint**, an anxious toll inspector following impossible orders and trying to limit harm.
The local beneficiary is **Rusk Pettifer**, who arranged the exchange so his freight network would
look indispensable. Rusk is not the top of the ladder: his written instructions came from Provost
Halwen Drail. None of that invalidates a victory in Bramblebridge.

The bell itself is not magical. A tiny protective charm in its crown merely keeps rainwater from
freezing in winter; it cannot ring the bell. Magic may reveal residue, emotion, or material history,
but it does not solve motive, timing, or responsibility by itself.

## Three-clue matrix

| Deduction | Physical clue | Social clue | Documentary/consequence clue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The wagon changed hands | dry flour beneath wet canvas | Turnip responds to two whistles but resents one | wheel timing leaves six quiet minutes unaccounted for |
| The bell event was staged | theatre-pulley fibres on the bell rope | Sera Lark recognizes a rehearsal knot | civic bell intervals do not match what dockworkers heard |
| The diversion used the river | blue river clay on one rear wheel | a barge child heard the pattern as a loading signal | quay tally has a scraped-out half-load |
| The new map concealed a route | alley sign moved one wall west | Tibb remembers the old lane exactly | the printed watch map and Fenna's gift map disagree |
| Coercion sits beneath the crime | Edra's torn debt notice | Fara's anger collapses when Edra is named | exemptions favor carriers tied to Rusk's network |

Never require all three clues in a row. Two clues justify a useful conclusion; the third should add
confidence, nuance, or leverage.

## Session 1 — Rain at Noon

### Opening read-aloud

> Rain had been polishing Bramblebridge since breakfast. It silvered the bridge rails, darkened the
> market awnings, and persuaded every dog in town to stand exactly where a hurried person meant to
> step. At noon the civic bell began its usual twelve strokes. The town counted with it. Then came a
> thirteenth—late, low, and somehow embarrassed. Through the arch rolled a tax wagon with no driver,
> one offended horse, and canvas that dripped like a roof. Someone in the crowd said “ghost.” Someone
> else said “audit.” The second word caused considerably more panic.

### Scene 1: The wagon enters

Give the table immediate, practical choices rather than an initiative demand:

- calm Turnip before the wagon strikes a stall;
- keep frightened people from crushing one another beneath wet awnings;
- protect the wagon without letting Inspector Fara seize every witness;
- follow someone leaving too quickly;
- watch the bell tower while everyone else watches the horse.

Success should establish goodwill. Failure should cause broken crockery, bruised dignity, a furious
goose, or a lost early lead—not death or campaign collapse. Merrit Vale offers towels and complains
that heroes always arrive before the floor is mopped.

### Scene 2: First impressions

**Tibb Fallow** declares himself “the Falcon of the Weir,” immediately slips on cabbage leaves, then
recovers by recalling the wagon's route turn by turn. Play the embarrassment briefly and let his
competence matter. He knows the wagon used an alley that does not exist on his new patrol map.

**Magistrate Elowen Pike** takes control with short sentences: “No seizure. No accusation. Names,
times, and dry paper first.” She gives the party temporary standing to investigate if they accept
three boundaries: no planted evidence, no compelled minds, and no punishment without a hearing.

**Fara Dint** is strict, damp, exhausted, and defensive. She wants the wagon inventoried immediately
because delay violates her orders. She is not cruel for sport. A character who offers her a lawful
way to pause earns the first sign that her orders set contradictory deadlines.

### Scene 3: The market investigation

Place at least three leads in plain sight:

1. Flour beneath the canvas is dry though the wagon bed is wet at the edges.
2. Turnip follows Tibb's ordinary whistle but flinches at a two-note dock whistle.
3. A rear wheel carries blue clay found near the quay, not on the bridge road.
4. One rope fibre caught on the tax seal is dyed theatre red.
5. A small chalk `XIII` under the axle is a loader's count, not an occult mark.

Creative solutions include weighing cargo, asking bakers about moisture, letting an animal-friendly
character observe Turnip, recreating the route in rain, borrowing a theatre lantern, questioning
children under stalls, or simply serving hot soup until witnesses stop performing certainty.

### Cozy close

At the Gilded Kettle, everyone contributes a contradictory version of the thirteenth stroke. Merrit
serves onion broth and labels each bowl according to witness reliability. End with Tibb unfolding
two maps: the official patrol copy and the hand-drawn gift map he is mortified to explain. One shows
a wall. The other shows Narrow Molly Lane.

### If the party leaves town

They can follow tracks or protect a suspected witness. Pike sends Tibb with them, and the next clue
appears through the changed map or Turnip's whistle. The market investigation continues without
them and produces the dry-flour report later.

## Session 2 — Above the Market

### Opening read-aloud

> Morning found the bell tower wearing a scarf of cloud. Below it, Bramblebridge resumed business
> with the grim courage of people who had already paid for stall space. Tibb waited at the tower door
> with three keys, two poems, and the expression of a man who had tested none of them. From somewhere
> above came a soft wooden knock. “That,” he said, “is either evidence or a pigeon with legal advice.”

### Scene 1: Entering the tower

The obvious stair is unsafe where a tread has split. The party may repair it, climb externally,
borrow theatre rigging, cross from the guild roof, send a small scout, question the bell keeper, or
investigate from below. Reward plans that protect townspeople and the ancient bell.

The tower contains:

- red pulley fibres caught where the civic rope was temporarily sleeved;
- a rehearsal knot Sera Lark teaches only to stop scenery dropping on actors;
- clean scuffs showing that a portable frame stood behind the louvers;
- wax in one groove, used to delay a return stroke;
- the harmless winter charm, visibly old and incapable of causing the event.

### Riddle: Thirteen without sorcery

On the bell keeper's timing board are four phrases:

> Twelve belong to the town.  
> One belongs to the river.  
> The town waits between names.  
> The river waits between loads.

The solution is not a password. The players should realize the first twelve strokes follow civic
spacing while the last intervals match a quay loading signal. They can solve it by listening,
measuring, asking a dockworker, moving cups on a table, performing the rhythm, or comparing witness
descriptions. If they do not solve it, a barge child taps the same rhythm while waiting for soup.

### Scene 2: The theatre

**Sera Lark** is laid-back until safety is questioned. She confirms the fibre and recognizes that a
portable thunder frame is missing. She refuses a search made merely to reassure officials, but
welcomes a practical inventory or a performance that flushes out whoever knows the substitute
storage place.

**Jory Fenner**, costume maker, is charming and anxious. He lent rain capes to “quay laborers” who
paid in exact change and returned one cape cleaner than it left. He remembers a blue-clay footprint.

Possible comic complication: the Wayward Company arrives to arrest the “Bell Phantom,” complete
with a net designed for something roughly bell-shaped. They are loud but useful. If treated kindly,
they create a public distraction while the party follows a quieter lead.

### Scene 3: The false arrest

A frightened apprentice is blamed because red fibre was found in her pocket. The fibre is ordinary
theatre waste, not cut from the treated pulley line. The party can expose the difference through
craft, witness sequence, kindness, or demonstrating that the apprentice could not reach the tower
and unload cargo within six minutes.

If the accusation hardens, Pike intervenes but loses political room. If the party protects the
apprentice with evidence, Fara begins sharing the contradictions in her orders.

### Cozy close

Sera stages a five-minute “historical reconstruction” in the Kettle using spoons, laundry line,
and the Wayward Company's net. It is ridiculous and surprisingly accurate. The reconstructed timing
leaves exactly six quiet minutes between the wagon leaving the quay and entering the market.

## Session 3 — Six Quiet Minutes

### Opening read-aloud

> Narrow Molly Lane had been removed from the new map with admirable neatness and no effect at all
> on the lane. It remained between a cooper's wall and a leaning row of sheds, smelling of wet oak,
> river clay, and onions. Tibb stood where the printed wall ought to be and looked personally
> betrayed by cartography. Somewhere behind the sheds, Turnip answered a two-note whistle.

### Scene 1: Reconstruct the exchange

The physical problem has four moving parts: wagon, substitute driver, cargo carriers, and bell
signal. Let players place tokens, people, cups, or chalk marks to test sequences. A valid theory must
fit these constraints:

- the wagon is unwatched for only six minutes;
- the bell signal tells carriers whether the quay route is clear;
- dry flour was placed under already-wet canvas;
- no witness saw both the bell operator and cargo exchange;
- Turnip knows Edra and the substitute by different whistles.

Several solutions identify the route. The exact division of labor can remain uncertain until a
witness speaks.

### Scene 2: Find Edra

Turnip, drainage water, lime dust, or a child's report leads to the jammed lime shed. Edra is cold,
angry, and alive. Opening the swollen door is a rescue problem: lever it without collapsing sacks,
drain water through a narrow runnel, enter through the roof, calm Edra before she pushes from within,
or recruit mill workers with proper braces.

Edra admits leaving the wagon but not willingly. Her brother's ferry debt was used as leverage. She
heard Fara argue against threats and believes Rusk's people changed the plan afterward. This creates
a choice: public testimony now, protected testimony later, a controlled lie to draw out the
substitute, or a direct approach to Rusk.

### Scene 3: Fara's decision

Fara arrives with authority to seize the wagon and Edra's statement. If confronted only with blame,
she becomes rigid. If shown the impossible timeline, altered order, or human cost, she admits she
was ordered to make local freight “demonstrably unreliable.” She can become witness, reluctant
opponent, or procedural shield depending on how the party treats her.

Rusk does not appear as a combat boss. He sends a courteous note offering to return the missing
cargo, cancel the ferry debt, and meet over supper—if the party describes which records they copied.
The offer proves reach, not guilt. Accepting, refusing, counteroffering, publicizing, or using it as a
decoy all open Q02–Q04 differently.

### Resolution choices

The party can truthfully establish the staged diversion while differing about disclosure:

- publish everything and force immediate inquiry;
- protect Edra while releasing material evidence;
- expose the local mechanism but conceal the higher lead temporarily;
- bargain for restitution and preserve a case against Drail;
- turn Fara into a witness and use her office to keep trade moving;
- create a public reconstruction that prevents officials from replacing evidence with rumor.

Each helps someone and costs something. None is the secret “correct ending.”

### Cozy return and fresh door

The town holds a late breakfast because nobody slept. Tibb reads a four-line poem; Fenna stops him
after line six. Turnip receives an apple and bites Rusk's note. In the tack, Brother Odo finds pale
orange fungus that should not grow in Alderwick. The bell mystery can close cleanly while the fungus
opens a genuinely unrelated Bellafont orchard story.

## Character hooks without prescribed backstories

Offer one hook per character and let the player accept, reject, or rewrite it:

- a craftsperson recognizes the pulley treatment or wagon repair;
- a legal or scholarly character has seen contradictory local orders before;
- a traveler once depended on an informal freight network like Rusk's;
- a religious character knows the Quiet Bell custom of pausing after an unexplained stroke;
- a performer knows how a crowd can be directed without every participant knowing the plan;
- a nature-minded character notices Turnip's trust, the wrong clay, or the foreign fungus;
- a character with debt or family obligations recognizes Edra's coercion before officials do;
- a magic user is asked to prove too much with magic and must explain its honest limits.

## Encounter guidance without invented mechanics

Use danger only when choices and environment justify it. Suitable opening pressures are a runaway
wagon, a crowded awning collapse, an unsafe tower stair, a swollen shed door, frightened animals,
or hired carriers trying to escape without killing anyone. Resolve them through the table's actual
D&D 2024 characters and existing rules owners. Do not assign numbers from this narrative packet.

## Between-session questions

After each session, ask one cozy question and one pressure question:

1. Who in Bramblebridge made you feel welcome? Who now thinks you are trouble?
2. What ordinary task did you help with? Which clue did you trust too quickly?
3. What part of town feels like yours now? What truth are you not ready to make public?
