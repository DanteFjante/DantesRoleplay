# Caldris expanded locations — second-ring gazetteer

Status: **review content; location names are not runtime IDs**
World context: [Caldris World bible](CALDRIS-WORLD-BIBLE.md)
Primary-city context: [Caldris gazetteer](CALDRIS-GAZETTEER.md)
Slice: [Caldris Authoring Slice 2](CALDRIS-AUTHORING-SLICE-2-IMPLEMENTATION.md)

These forty-eight locations sit beyond the primary cities and first-campaign settlements. Each has
a material reason to exist, a memorable everyday texture, a current pressure, a hidden layer, and
at least one playable use. They add breadth without making every road lead back to one conspiracy.

## Eredane

### Alderwick

| Location | Material purpose and texture | Present pressure | Hidden layer | Story use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sourapple Ford** | A shallow cattle crossing, cider hamlet, and eel-smoking stop where every fence leans in the same prevailing wind. | A new upstream weir has deepened the ford and stranded the autumn drove. | The submerged older road bears Lantern Concord mile stones deliberately turned face-down. | Negotiate water use, move a herd creatively, or trace who concealed the road markers. |
| **The Button Hills** | Chalk sheep country dotted with round barrows and generations of lost coat buttons gathered by children after rain. | Sinkholes are opening along the wool road. | The “barrows” are collapsed storehouses from a pre-kingdom famine network, not tombs. | Rescue trapped shepherds, map safe paths, and decide who may claim the recovered grain seals. |
| **Mallow Quay** | A small river landing for honey, medicinal herbs, and cheap pottery, perfumed by marsh mallow and woodsmoke. | Barges refuse to stop after three crews report a singing light beneath the pilings. | A retired lamp-keeper is using an old resonant vessel to warn of illegal dredging. | Unmask a supposed haunting without humiliating its well-intentioned author. |
| **King's Patience Wood** | Managed oak woodland supplying bridge beams, charcoal, pannage, and the kingdom's slowest official hunting lodge. | Foresters and villagers dispute emergency cutting after storm damage. | Several marked royal oaks are young substitutions hiding an old steward's careful theft. | Solve a timber audit, survive comic court hospitality, or protect evidence before the trees are felled. |

### Dunmarrow

| Location | Material purpose and texture | Present pressure | Hidden layer | Story use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Blackcap Fell** | Peat-cutting upland of black stone shelters, hard rain, and tiny white flowers used in miners' funerals. | A peat fire burns underground and vents beside isolated homes. | Heat has revealed an older ritual road aligned to no present fortress. | Coordinate evacuation, smother the fire, and learn why soldiers once marched away from the border. |
| **Latchwater** | Reservoir village maintaining sluices for mines and winter mills; every doorway displays a carved wooden latch. | Someone opens the upper gate at night, wasting scarce water. | The culprit is diverting flow to expose a shaft where unpaid workers were sealed during a past collapse. | Balance water survival against historical justice and a dangerous recovery. |
| **Saint Vey's Crutch** | A narrow pass with an inn built around a bent pine said to have supported a wandering saint. | An avalanche has closed the official road while an escort company monopolizes the safer goat path. | The innkeeper knows a miners' ventilation route but fears it will become a smuggling tunnel. | Break a travel monopoly through negotiation, engineering, or trust rather than combat alone. |
| **Rook Anvil** | A forge hamlet recycling worn tools and battlefield iron beneath a rook colony that mimics hammer rhythms. | Finished blades crack despite good workmanship. | Scrap dealers mixed in brittle metal from a Concord device whose maker's marks were filed away. | Trace a supply chain and prevent blame from ruining an innocent smithing family. |

### Carrowmere

| Location | Material purpose and texture | Present pressure | Hidden layer | Story use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Brinewife's Steps** | Salt terraces descending to tidal pans, worked by extended households in mirrored light. | An unusual tide spoils the season's first harvest. | A collapsed sea wall reveals a shrine recording a forgotten rescue compact with Selucian sailors. | Repair the pans, interpret the compact, and decide whether an old debt remains binding. |
| **Mackerel Crown** | A crowded fishing island whose houses wear blue roof tiles salvaged from wrecks. | Two elected harbour speakers both possess apparently valid seals. | Neither seal is false; an old charter intentionally created dual authority during plague years. | Keep the fleet sailing while inventing a fair temporary government. |
| **The Dry Admiral** | Inland ship-timber yard and tavern where retired captains command imaginary fleets over bowls of stew. | Carefully seasoned masts disappear without wagon tracks. | River thieves float them away through a forgotten flood channel at night. | Stage a decoy mast, follow the channel, or recruit argumentative retirees for surveillance. |
| **Gannet Mercy** | Cliff hospice and signal station caring for wreck survivors regardless of flag. | A rescued stranger refuses to identify a ship no lookout saw sink. | The “wreck” was a covert prisoner transfer interrupted by a real storm. | Protect a frightened witness while separating maritime law, espionage, and simple compassion. |

### Bellafont

| Location | Material purpose and texture | Present pressure | Hidden layer | Story use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Quince Ladder** | Terraced orchard village reached by a stair-road wide enough for one mule and two strongly held opinions. | Fruit trees flower twice and exhaust themselves before harvest. | A buried warm-water conduit from the River Thrones era has reopened. | Redirect ancient irrigation without destroying orchards or awakening ownership claims. |
| **Blue Apron Fair** | Seasonal cloth-and-food market held in roofed lanes; stallholders wear indigo aprons to show neutral trading status. | A ceremonial apron has been stolen before treaty negotiations. | It was borrowed by a nervous apprentice hiding a repair that reveals an older royal emblem. | Recover the symbol discreetly and decide whether its hidden heraldry matters. |
| **Mothglass House** | Rural workshop producing fine window glass and maintaining mulberry hedges for silk moths. | Night moths gather in impossible numbers around one furnace. | Their migration follows mineral traces in sand taken from an illicit excavation. | Follow the moths, settle a guild dispute, and choose whether to expose the excavation. |
| **The Rain Parlour** | A roadside pavilion where orchard workers wait out storms, exchange songs, and leave jars of preserved fruit. | Someone replaces friendly messages with threatening prophecies. | A lonely child copies fragments from genuine old court letters without understanding them. | Prevent panic, find the source gently, and recognize a useful historical fragment. |

### Lornesse

| Location | Material purpose and texture | Present pressure | Hidden layer | Story use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Seven Echo Quarry** | Slate quarry where each work call returns in a distinct echo used to measure the cliff. | One echo has vanished, making blasting unsafe. | A newly opened chamber absorbs sound and contains woven copper curtains. | Explore through touch and signals, then decide whether mining may safely continue. |
| **Goatminster** | High pasture village whose elected goat judge settles grazing disputes beneath a painted canopy. | Prize animals vanish and return wearing unfamiliar bells. | A secluded household across the ridge is trading for breeding stock without legal crossing rights. | Turn apparent theft into a border and kinship negotiation. |
| **Warmstone Baths** | Modest mineral baths used by miners, pilgrims, aching nobles, and relentless local gossips. | The spring cools during the busiest healing season. | Heat is being diverted by a reopened copper gallery beneath an abandoned hospice. | Restore water, rescue illicit miners, and expose the investor without collapsing the baths. |
| **The Widow's Incline** | A steep freight railway of ropes, brakes, and timber sledges run by a cooperative of miners' families. | Brake ropes have been cut three times. | The sabotage aims to halt shipments long enough to conceal an unsafe royal inspection. | Protect a descent, inspect the mechanism, and uncover who profits from delay. |

### Veyr Marches

| Location | Material purpose and texture | Present pressure | Hidden layer | Story use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ninepost Field** | Neutral meadow where nine carved posts mark grazing, court, market, and muster rights for different marches. | A tenth post appears overnight with convincing weathering. | It was carved decades ago for a march that was legally erased after rebellion. | Determine authenticity and choose whether historical suppression grants present rights. |
| **Wheelwife Hollow** | Wagon-repair settlement built around a spring and a circular common where axle-makers test their work. | Travelers arrive with wheels failing in the same peculiar way. | An escort company salts ruts with sharp forge waste to create demand. | Prove commercial sabotage through roadcraft, witnesses, or a harmless counter-trap. |
| **The Courteous Ruin** | Roofless border manor where travelers still follow elaborate guest customs preserved by its caretaker family. | Armed claimants demand the keys to a house with no doors. | The ruin's hospitality register proves ancestors from rival marches sheltered one another during war. | Host a tense meal, protect the ledger, and make courtesy politically consequential. |
| **Foxglove Relay** | Hilltop courier station breeding small hardy horses and drying purple flowers for safe medicinal trade. | Messages arrive resealed but apparently unread. | A clerk is copying only wax impressions to reconstruct who corresponds with whom. | Catch metadata espionage before false accusations cause a march feud. |

## Solasca

### Ordelain

| Location | Material purpose and texture | Present pressure | Hidden layer | Story use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Bent Mile** | Riverbank farming strip where every boundary curves around a channel that no longer exists. | Surveyors arrive to straighten titles and displace tenant families. | The curves preserve the route of an ancient flood escape canal. | Defend practical memory against tidy law before the next high water. |
| **Inkfish Wharf** | Mud-brick landing for scribes' ink, fish oil, reeds, and legal couriers, notorious for black-stained cats. | Official documents fade to blank within days. | A merchant substituted reactive lampblack to ruin evidence in a navigation lawsuit. | Reconstruct vanished texts from copies, impressions, and remembered phrasing. |
| **Pomegranate Court** | Shaded caravan court where fruit sellers, petition writers, and musicians share a long cistern. | The cistern tastes of copper and rumors blame a curse. | New royal pipes cross an older bronze memorial conduit and are slowly corroding. | Fix a public-health problem while determining why the memorial was omitted from plans. |
| **Reed Judge's Rest** | Small island courthouse and ferry inn that hears cases when the Aur changes local boundaries. | The island is drifting downstream faster than precedent allows. | Its foundations are a deliberately detachable River Thrones tribunal platform. | Save records, hold an absurd jurisdiction hearing, and decide where the court legally belongs. |

### Namarra

| Location | Material purpose and texture | Present pressure | Hidden layer | Story use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Three Spoon Village** | Delta settlement famous for communal kitchens and three enormous wooden serving spoons carried during floods. | One neighbourhood refuses the evacuation signal. | Its elders know the assigned refuge was quietly sold to a warehouse company. | Organize a safe alternative and expose a paper crime before floodwater arrives. |
| **Mosquito Abbey** | Raised monastery studying fever seasons, drainage, and inexpensive nets amid relentless buzzing. | Stored remedies are missing while nearby families fall ill. | A novice distributes them secretly because official charity rules exclude unregistered villages. | Protect lives first, then reform or confront the rule without scapegoating the novice. |
| **The Saffron Ox** | Dry-season cattle market and dye stop with orange awnings visible for miles across the flats. | A prized breeding ox apparently predicts which bidder will die. | Its reactions track a scent found on poisoned account tablets. | Solve a theatrical “prophecy” and a very practical attempted murder. |
| **Drowned Door Orchard** | Fruit trees grow on narrow levees around the upper frame of a submerged stone doorway. | Divers seeking treasure destabilize the levee. | The doorway belonged to a public granary whose remaining chambers may still hold seed jars. | Balance archaeology, food security, and flood engineering. |

## Second-ring use rules

- Reveal only the places approached by travel, relationships, trade, history, or consequences.
- A hidden layer is a GM-facing possibility for review, not automatically true in live play.
- Material pressures should continue if ignored, but not every delay becomes catastrophe.
- Local people retain agency and useful expertise; adventurers make decisive differences without
  becoming the only competent people in Caldris.
- Exact containment, adjacency, travel time, map placement, and visibility require future confirmed
  runtime owners.
