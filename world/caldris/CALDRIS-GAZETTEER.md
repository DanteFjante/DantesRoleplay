# Caldris gazetteer

Status: **expanded review content; locations are not runtime IDs**
World context: [Caldris world bible](CALDRIS-WORLD-BIBLE.md)

This gazetteer gives all thirty-six primary cities a reason to exist, a playable identity, a source
of warmth, and a current pressure. Smaller places supply roads, ruins, villages, crossings, wild
areas, and adventure sites. Editorial keys such as `Alderwick / Bramblebridge` are human review
labels only.

## Eredane

### Alderwick

#### Merecross — capital at three waters

Merecross grew where the Mere meets the Lark and Weir rivers. Barges, paper, apples, wool, court
business, and bridge tolls feed a city of stone quays and timber upper stories. Its seven public
bridges are maintained by different institutions, producing beautiful views and spectacularly
petty jurisdiction disputes.

- **Districts:** Crown Hill, Paperbank, Old Weir, South Quays, Lantern Close, Bakers' Reach.
- **Warmth anchor:** the Steaming Cup arcade, where petitioners share long tables and court clerks
  quietly explain forms to frightened visitors.
- **Public tension:** new “temporary” toll schedules change faster than merchants can print them.
- **Hidden tension:** several toll exemptions carry counterfeit Concord seals accepted by senior
  officials who know they are false.
- **Adventure entries:** a bridge bell rings from an empty tower; an inheritance map uses a river
  channel that vanished two centuries ago; a royal comedy troupe loses a politically sensitive
  prop crown.

#### Highmead — wool, schools, and windy opinions

Highmead stands on a defensible chalk ridge above sheep country. It is known for wool halls,
teacher-priests, legal schools, large skies, and citizens who regard climbing the hill as evidence
of moral seriousness.

- **Districts:** Lower Steps, Scholars' Row, Fullers' Bank, Whitewall, East Fold.
- **Warmth anchor:** the Common Oven, maintained by three rival parishes that agree only on bread.
- **Public tension:** students and fullers compete for clean river water.
- **Hidden tension:** a respected lecturer has found evidence that the Crown's preferred succession
  interpretation is only forty years old.
- **Adventure entries:** missing examination seals; a debating society accidentally challenges a
  real magistrate; sheep refuse one ancient field.

#### Dunwarren — forest gate and royal timber town

Dunwarren controls the eastern forest roads and supplies beams, charcoal, carts, and bow staves.
Its walls incorporate living oaks protected by charter, making maintenance both impressive and
botanically argumentative.

- **Districts:** Green Gate, Cartwrights' End, Royal Yard, Barkmarket, Warren Close.
- **Warmth anchor:** the Sawyers' Bathhouse, cheap, loud, and neutral ground for labour disputes.
- **Public tension:** royal timber quotas exceed regrowth estimates.
- **Hidden tension:** marked trees form part of an older boundary compact with Mourenne Under-Mere.
- **Adventure entries:** falsified felling marks; a bell-fox den beneath the armoury; a surveyor who
  returned with someone else's boots and no memory of changing them.

### Deep starting settlements

#### Bramblebridge — first-campaign market town

Bramblebridge sits one day east of Merecross where the old Lantern Road crosses the narrow Weir.
It serves twelve villages, three mills, Mallow Abbey, and a weekly market famous for onions, wool,
second-hand books, and disputes over stall awnings. The bridge is broad enough for two carts but
rarely for two opinions.

- **Neighbourhoods:** Bridge End, Bell Square, Tanners' Lane, Abbey Road, Little Quay.
- **Warmth anchor:** the Gilded Kettle inn, where supper is served family-style and the owner can
  remember what everyone claimed not to like.
- **Public tension:** a new toll inspector insists every chicken transported “with commercial
  intent” requires separate declaration.
- **Hidden tension:** the inspector's orders are designed to create desperation that a smuggling
  ring can exploit.
- **Opening image:** rain shines on the bridge stones while the noon bell rings thirteen times and
  a tax wagon arrives without its driver.

#### Candlefen — reeds, ferries, and unhurried expertise

Candlefen spreads along raised paths and low islands where the Weir enters a broad marsh. It makes
reed mats, candles, smoked eel, and excellent shallow boats. Outsiders mistake its unhurried speech
for laziness until floodwater rises.

- **Warmth anchor:** Nessa Quill's ferry shelter, always supplied with tea, dry socks, and gossip
  whose accuracy improves with the weather.
- **Pressure:** drainage investors promise farmland while fen households warn that the channels
  protect Bramblebridge from floods.
- **Secret:** one channel follows a submerged Concord boundary and leads toward Mourenne's old
  grove.

#### Wren's Hollow — quarry village under singing cliffs

Wren's Hollow supplies pale building stone and roof slate. Wind moving through abandoned quarry
holes creates flute-like notes that villagers interpret with cheerful inconsistency.

- **Warmth anchor:** the communal stew shed beside the quarry lift.
- **Pressure:** a reopened face produces beautiful stone and inexplicable warm air.
- **Secret:** the quarry intersects an archive escape tunnel, not a natural cavern.

### Dunmarrow

#### Greyharrow

The capital crowns a black basalt ridge above two peat rivers. Its fortress, iron exchange, winter
courts, and roofed market make it severe from a distance; inside, painted doors, heated benches,
and competitive soup reveal a more domestic city.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Long Bench kitchens, which feed miners' families during closures.
- **Pressure:** the Crown wants new fortress taxes as mine revenue falls.
- **Adventure use:** a funeral choir competition masks a witness transfer; an obsolete mine token
  buys entry to a sealed Concord shaft.

#### Stonewake

Stonewake is a northern port built behind a long breakwater of fitted dark stone. Ore, smoked fish,
timber, and soldiers pass through it; so do rumours from beyond the Greyward Ocean.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Netmenders' Hall, where anyone who helps repair storm gear eats free.
- **Pressure:** warm water rises near the breakwater in midwinter.
- **Adventure use:** a lighthouse keeps signaling after evacuation; sailors return a chest that
  appears on no manifest.

#### Moorwatch

Moorwatch began as a frontier fort and became a borough of peat cutters, shepherds, horse breeders,
and military pensioners. Its straight streets end abruptly in bog.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Pensioners' Garden, guarded more seriously than the armoury.
- **Pressure:** fortress families want a standing army while townspeople fear another generation of
  permanent requisitions.
- **Adventure use:** a drill unit disappears in familiar fog; a strict captain needs outsiders to
  prove her own quartermaster is honest.

### Carrowmere

#### Saltmere

Saltmere stands on tidal islands joined by stone causeways and movable timber bridges. Royal offices
share space with fish markets, salt warehouses, rescue houses, and legal chambers specialising in
things that floated away.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Night Kettle rescue kitchen beside East Gate.
- **Pressure:** the shipwright strike threatens autumn grain convoys.
- **Adventure use:** a wreck survivor is recognized by two families; a royal barge is stolen at low
  tide and found upstream.

#### Gullstrand

Gullstrand's vast yards build and repair deep-water ships. The city smells of oak, tar, hot metal,
salt, and communal kitchens run by craft fraternities.

- **Warmth anchor:** Dock Nine's shared nursery and meal hall.
- **Pressure:** preventable injuries have become the focus of a principled labour stoppage.
- **Adventure use:** sabotage is blamed on strikers; a half-built hull contains timber from a
  protected Alderwick grove.

#### Penross

Penross clings to western cliffs around a natural harbour and a renowned school of navigation,
weather, law, and rescue medicine.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Window Library, where ship journals are read aloud on storm nights.
- **Pressure:** an admired professor sells confident but falsified seasonal forecasts.
- **Adventure use:** cliff bells ring in clear weather; a student map depicts an island no captain
  admits visiting.

### Bellafont

#### Bellafont

The capital terraces down orchard hills toward a palace, cathedral gardens, cloth halls, fountains,
and a river lined with laundry platforms. Manners are polished; civic debates are not.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Pear Court, where surplus fruit becomes preserves for winter charity.
- **Pressure:** the court minimizes pale-canker while orchard villages cut century-old trees.
- **Adventure use:** an amateur play repeats a private royal conversation; irrigation gates open
  with no keeper present.

#### Vintner's Reach

Vintner's Reach controls southern wine roads, barrel making, river barges, and hillside reservoirs.
Its guild feasts are legendary and its hangovers legally irrelevant.

- **Warmth anchor:** cooper families maintain the Travellers' Vat, a vast bath and laundry.
- **Pressure:** monasteries and town vintners contest water during drought.
- **Adventure use:** a wine tastes of a vanished orchard; barrel marks encode land claims.

#### Cresset

Cresset is a textile and dye city where canals turn fulling mills and entire streets change colour
with the day's work.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Washday Steps, home to songs, snacks, and union announcements.
- **Pressure:** a brilliant new dye makes workers ill while its patron denies the recipe changed.
- **Adventure use:** blue footprints cross a locked archive; a guild mascot is mistaken for a
  supernatural omen.

### Lornesse

#### Lorn

Lorn fills a hanging valley beneath the royal monastery-fortress. Bells coordinate markets,
avalanches, funerals, fire, court sessions, and lunch with patterns outsiders routinely confuse.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Underbell arcade, warm in winter and full of soup sellers.
- **Pressure:** the young king's regency attracts flattering “advisers” carrying old debt claims.
- **Adventure use:** a bell pattern orders evacuation from an empty valley; a comic bell rehearsal
  uncovers a real alarm.

#### Kestrelwatch

This pass city controls caravans, mountain rescue, and the safest eastern crossing. Houses cluster
behind snow walls and connect through covered lanes.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Oath Rope lodge, where rescued travellers add a knot and promise future aid.
- **Pressure:** competing escorts hide route conditions to protect business.
- **Adventure use:** a rescue call comes from a closed pass; Oriselle's network is impersonated.

#### Copperdeep

Copperdeep descends through terraces around ancient mine mouths, smelters, baths, chapels, and
workers' halls. Upper galleries are active; lower levels have been sealed for generations.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Third Shift baths and midnight breakfast market.
- **Pressure:** a new ventilation draft and fresh soot appear below the Concord seal.
- **Adventure use:** miners hear formal knocking; a tithe niche fits an object larger than a wagon.

### Veyr Marches

#### Redgate

Redgate surrounds the rust-red stone arch where nine march roads meet. The High Warden's moot,
escort market, banner court, and sprawling inns make it a capital only when everyone agrees.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Nine Pots kitchen, one stew contribution from each march.
- **Pressure:** succession delegates arrive with incompatible procedures.
- **Adventure use:** someone steals only the blank spaces from heraldic records; a shepherd becomes
  the unwilling favourite candidate.

#### Vanner's Rest

Vanner's Rest grew around caravan fields and is famous for wheelwrights, horse doctors, roadside
theatre, and the loudest inn signs in Eredane.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Free Axle yard, where stranded travellers work for repairs.
- **Pressure:** escort companies stage false raids to justify higher fees.
- **Adventure use:** a rival adventuring company takes the wrong rescue contract; an actor inherits
  a watchtower.

#### Oakshield

Oakshield guards forest passes with layered timber walls designed to be repaired, not admired. It
exports horses, charcoal, leather, and excellent preserved mushrooms.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Foal House, a stable and community meeting room.
- **Pressure:** a border-mapping commission treats seasonal grazing routes as permanent frontiers.
- **Adventure use:** vanished boundary posts reappear in a circle; a strict ranger and laid-back
  charcoal burner each know half the truth.

## Solasca

### Ordelain

#### Aurent

Aurent spans both banks of the Aur with broad quays, palaces, law schools, grain exchanges, shaded
courts, and public fountains. River cases can make or end political careers.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Evening Steps, where storytellers, clerks, and boat families share food.
- **Pressure:** the changing river exposes pre-Concord foundations beneath a ducal estate.
- **Adventure use:** three courts issue valid orders over one sandbar; a legal manuscript predicts
  its own theft.

#### Vessa

Vessa makes paper from river reeds and houses private, royal, temple, and commercial archives. Its
streets are shaded by cloth awnings and patrolled aggressively for fire.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Copyists' Tea Court, open all night before filing deadlines.
- **Pressure:** one archive buys documents that should legally be returned to villages.
- **Adventure use:** blank pages reveal text when carried upriver; an eccentric forger asks the
  party to prove her innocent of a forgery she admires.

#### Malvar

Malvar is the kingdom's granary city and cattle market, built around enormous brick storehouses and
a temple distribution complex.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Open Ladle kitchens, funded by market fines.
- **Pressure:** reported reserves and physical grain do not match.
- **Adventure use:** grain sacks contain river clay; a warehouse cat becomes a union symbol.

### Namarra

#### Namar

Namar rises on islands and embankments where the delta branches. Boats are ordinary transport;
addresses include both street and nearest landing.

- **Warmth anchor:** floating breakfast markets that tie together each dawn.
- **Pressure:** whole villages disappear from tax rolls but not from supply chains.
- **Adventure use:** a clerk insists a living witness is legally a canal; puppeteers encode a
  missing-village list into comedy.

#### Reedcrown

Reedcrown guards royal granaries, seed stores, canal gates, and flood reserves behind immense reed
and brick embankments.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Seed Mothers' courtyard and free gardening lessons.
- **Pressure:** someone substitutes short-lived seed while selling the durable stock abroad.
- **Adventure use:** a granary lock opens during a funeral; lantern herons nest in a forbidden
  archive roof.

#### Saffron Quay

Saffron Quay is a warm-water export port for spice, cloth, rice, fish, and delta craft. Warehouses
stand on pilings, and tide marks are painted as civic records.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Pilots' Supper, free to any crew that helped another vessel that month.
- **Pressure:** shell merchants move grain from villages the state denies exist.
- **Adventure use:** a cargo owner has no legal name; a black scale is used as a warehouse scoop.

### Tessarane

#### Tessa

Tessa is a seasonal royal city of pale stone, wind courts, horse markets, observatories, and broad
public cisterns. The court moves here for clear summer skies.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Night Roofs, where families picnic during meteor showers.
- **Pressure:** three court factions claim the same harmless comet legitimizes them.
- **Adventure use:** a prophecy contains a bookkeeping error; a royal astrologer needs protection
  from admirers, not assassins.

#### Orovant

Orovant makes glass, lenses, instruments, and blue ceramics using desert-edge sand and caravan fuel.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Shaded Kiln school for apprentices injured or displaced at work.
- **Pressure:** fuel demand strips protective scrub and drives glass-locust swarms.
- **Adventure use:** a lens shows writing that is not present; an eccentric instrument maker has
  sold the same telescope to four nobles.

#### Blue Step

Blue Step sits where the plateau road descends through blue-grey cliffs toward the Lantern Sea. It
is a caravan, customs, and animal-remount city.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Long Shade colonnade and travellers' kitchens.
- **Pressure:** customs officers seize “prophetic” goods inconsistently and auction them privately.
- **Adventure use:** a camel carries a sealed diplomatic saddle with no rider; a staircase count
  changes after rain.

### Selucia

#### Candleport

Candleport climbs around a protected harbour beneath the royal citadel. Pilgrim bells, fishing
boats, citrus markets, coffee houses, embassies, and parliament stairs crowd the waterfront.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Dawn Cups, small coffee stalls that collectively feed shipwreck survivors.
- **Pressure:** royal wedding preparations conceal labour and island-right disputes.
- **Adventure use:** the wedding cook vanishes; a ceremonial boat is seaworthy but historically
  fraudulent.

#### Mariselle

Mariselle is an artistic and commercial port of tile workshops, theatres, rope yards, music schools,
and crowded hillside neighbourhoods.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Open Balcony concerts after the evening heat.
- **Pressure:** theatre patrons use rival productions to conduct maritime negotiations.
- **Adventure use:** a prop sea monster contains a real smuggling compartment; an actor speaks lines
  from a play not yet written.

#### Saint Orro

Saint Orro surrounds a fortified pilgrimage monastery and the islands' largest repair dock. The
saint is remembered for rescuing enemies and criticizing their knots.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Pilgrim Washhouse, neutral ground for crews and travellers.
- **Pressure:** a relic dispute may close the dock during storm season.
- **Adventure use:** Tavaros returns a future ship's compass; a strict prior and laid-back dockmaster
  must cooperate.

### Kethria

#### Var Keth

The capital occupies terraces above the confluence of three snowmelt canals. Copper roofs, carved
doors, council courts, wrestling grounds, and fruit gardens define the city.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Evening Channels, opened for children and washing after work.
- **Pressure:** a fraudulent office doubles the mountain copper tithe.
- **Adventure use:** royal seals appear on ingots cast before the current dynasty; canal water runs
  warm at midnight.

#### Copperwell

Copperwell serves mines, smelters, metalworkers, and caravan brokers. Its deep communal well is
lined with donation plaques spanning four centuries.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Hammer Meal, a rotating supper hosted by workshops.
- **Pressure:** refined copper disappears between assay and tithe storage.
- **Adventure use:** embercats lead apprentices to a hidden warm wall; a polite ceremonial argument
  is interrupted by a real theft.

#### Snowgate

Snowgate controls the northern pass and its avalanche shelters. Summer markets are brief and
intense; winter life moves into connected stone halls.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Red Room, kept heated for anyone stranded regardless of status.
- **Pressure:** someone removes rescue caches and replaces them with sealed copper boxes.
- **Adventure use:** a rescue bell rings beneath snow; Oriselle's oath knots appear in Kethrian
  patterns.

### Seven Lamps League

#### Aster Quay

Aster Quay is the League's diplomatic and financial centre, built around seven harbour basins and
the sealed hall where the Lantern Concord ended. Printing houses, embassies, insurers, warehouses,
and cafes fill narrow streets.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Common Table exchange, where every member city funds one dish.
- **Pressure:** Chair Ansel Daro vanished with an archive key and left three resignation letters.
- **Adventure use:** the sealed hall receives fresh flowers from inside; a debt collector tries to
  forgive a debt no living court recognizes.

#### Glassharbor

Glassharbor produces windows, mirrors, lenses, beads, bottles, and elaborate navigational lights.
Its furnaces glow along a sheltered bay.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Cullet Garden, made from safe recycled glass and tended by retired workers.
- **Pressure:** cheap imported fuel undermines forest agreements and guild safety.
- **Adventure use:** mirrors show different harbour flags; an apprentice finds a dragon joke etched
  inside flawless glass.

#### Low Lantern

Low Lantern occupies marshy islands at the northern strait. Warehouses, rope bridges, customs
houses, and cheap lodging make it less grand and more useful than the other League cities.

- **Warmth anchor:** the Dry Sock Fund, a dockworkers' institution with fierce accounting.
- **Pressure:** competing states impose overlapping “emergency” inspections.
- **Adventure use:** a customs dog inherits a warehouse; a ferry carries passengers who all claim
  not to know one another and share the same seal.

## Gazetteer use rules

- Primary cities are prepared at adventure-entry depth, not complete street-by-street simulation.
- Bramblebridge, Candlefen, Wren's Hollow, and their immediate sites are the first deep play region.
- Additional interiors, routes, and NPC presences are authored when a reviewed adventure requires
  them.
- Map illustrations must preserve broad relative geography but do not create canonical coordinates,
  distances, routes, borders, or discoveries.
