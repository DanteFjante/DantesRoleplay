# Caldris quest atlas

Status: **thirty review-content quest packets; not runtime quest records**
Campaign: [The Measure of Mercy](CALDRIS-CAMPAIGN-TAPESTRY.md)
World: [Caldris world bible](CALDRIS-WORLD-BIBLE.md)
Second-ring continuation: [Additional quests Q31–Q48](CALDRIS-ADDITIONAL-QUESTS.md)

Every packet has exactly three initial objectives for compatibility with the current bounded quest
shape. “Optional” is an editorial recommendation, not a runtime state. Each packet includes multiple
approaches, clue redundancy, recoverable failure, and an aftermath or handoff. Later creation must
resolve all references and visibility through actual owners.

## Volume I — Thirteen Bells

### Q01 — The Thirteenth Bell

**Hook:** Bramblebridge's noon bell rings thirteen times while an empty tax wagon rolls into market
with dry cargo in heavy rain.

**Layers:** apparent prank or haunting → missing driver and altered bell rope → coordinated diversion
for a selective cargo exchange.

**Objectives:**

1. Account for the wagon, driver, horse, cargo, and bell-tower access.
2. Identify how the bell rang and why witnesses heard different intervals.
3. Decide what to disclose before market panic or official seizure hardens the story.

**Routes:** tower inspection, animal handling, witness comparison, river mud, market records,
theatre rigging knowledge, magical investigation, or following exchanged cargo.

**Clues:** cut rope fibers on a theatre pulley; dry flour beneath wet canvas; the horse follows a
different driver's whistle; Tibb remembers a closed alley on the new map; bell timing matches a
barge signal, not a civic pattern.

**Creative constraints:** the bell must physically move or be convincingly imitated; the wagon
exchange needed six quiet minutes; no single witness saw both events.

**Failure forward:** public confusion gives Rusk's carriers time to leave, but their rushed route
creates clearer tracks and social resentment rather than ending the investigation.

**Aftermath:** resolving the event earns local trust; the horse's fungus-stained tack opens a later,
unrelated orchard thread.

### Q02 — Chickens of Commercial Intent

**Hook:** Inspector Fara Dint classifies market poultry one bird at a time, seizing a widow's cart
while favoured carriers pass under obscure exemptions.

**Layers:** absurd official → impossible revenue target and selective paperwork → Drail's policy
designed to make central freight control attractive.

**Objectives:**

1. Keep the market functioning without humiliating or assaulting vulnerable officials.
2. Compare enforcement against written orders and actual exemption use.
3. Secure practical relief for small traders while preserving evidence of higher direction.

**Routes:** legal challenge, comic public demonstration, alternate crossing, negotiation, evidence
collection, market strike, paying under protest, or exposing exempt cargo.

**Clues:** three versions of the same order; identical handwriting in unrelated exemption notes;
Fara's private marginal joke names the contradiction; Bridge House receipts show a revenue target
set before the rule existed.

**Riddle:** a guild proverb asks, “When is one goose three?” Answers grounded in the records include
owner, cargo, and meal; the intended lesson is that categories serve decisions, not truth.

**Failure forward:** seizures proceed for a day, generating an appeal record and new allies.

**Aftermath:** a grateful farmer pays with the three-claimant brooch, beginning a separate inheritance
problem.

### Q03 — The Wheel at Midnight

**Hook:** Old Weir Mill stops at the same minute every night although the river runs steadily.

**Layers:** haunting or sabotage → hidden sluice use → deed boundary and smuggling route intersect
with an older flood-control channel.

**Objectives:**

1. Protect workers and grain while testing the stoppage.
2. Trace the water or mechanism change without destroying the mill.
3. Settle immediate access to the sluice before harvest flour spoils.

**Routes:** mechanical inspection, timed watch, upstream survey, underwater approach, historical
deed research, negotiation with Jory, controlled shutdown, or magical observation.

**Clues:** polished wear on a disused gate; eel baskets displaced nightly; flour on a supposedly
flooded stair; Nan's copy and Della's deed omit the same boundary phrase; Jory owns a key but not the
only key.

**Creative constraints:** stopping the mill for long harms the town; forcing the gate risks flood;
the route also serves legitimate emergency drainage.

**Failure forward:** flour is lost and prices rise temporarily, but the visible shortage forces
authorities to hear the deed dispute.

**Aftermath:** repairs require Lornesse bell-metal stamped with a counterfeit rescue knot.

### Q04 — The Respectable Smuggler

**Hook:** Evidence points to Rusk Pettifer, who invites the party to supper and presents receipts for
medicine, winter tools, and flood relief his illegal network supplied.

**Layers:** charming benefactor → coercive logistics boss → regional provost uses Rusk to produce
selective disorder while planning to discard him.

**Objectives:**

1. Establish Rusk's harmful acts and socially useful services separately.
2. Prevent retaliation against witnesses, inspectors, and dependent households.
3. Defeat, expose, bargain with, displace, or turn Rusk while proposing a practical freight path.

**Routes:** infiltrate accounts, negotiate immunity, build a guild cooperative, controlled sting,
public hearing, seize routes, recruit Jory, or split Rusk's lieutenants.

**Clues:** payments to widows; threats in different handwriting; route changes matching Drail's
orders; a locked case with higher-level instructions; Merrit and Odo independently remember Rusk's
past flood aid.

**Failure forward:** Rusk escapes or retains leverage, but local coercion can still be disrupted and
his superior becomes wary.

**Aftermath:** Wayward Company arrives with a contract for “the respectable smuggler,” meaning a
different person in another shire.

## Volume II — The Price of a Fair Road

### Q05 — A Standard with a Hollow Heart

**Hook:** Fenna discovers that one official market weight is hollow and contains a strip of names,
debts, dates, and bridge symbols.

**Layers:** guild fraud → covert carrier preference list → old Concord notation revived for modern
toll coordination.

**Objectives:**

1. Authenticate the weight, insertion date, and custody history.
2. Decode enough entries to protect named innocents and identify active carriers.
3. Decide how to challenge guild complicity without destroying trust in every measure.

**Routes:** metallurgy, tool-mark comparison, archive research, surveillance, interviewing retired
assessors, controlled false entry, or Pella's repair expertise.

**Clues:** newer solder under old patina; maker's mark from Glassharbor; one name corresponds to a
dead road; recurring seven-place spacing; Fenna's family record explains access but not motive.

**Riddle:** “What grows lighter when filled?” The physical answer is the hollow weight when filled
with debt exemptions; the social answer is an obligation shifted onto others. Either insight points
to the list's purpose.

**Failure forward:** public disclosure causes market distrust, creating more witnesses and economic
cost rather than blocking the trail.

**Aftermath:** one innocent family asks help with a truly unrelated disputed roofline.

### Q06 — The Provost's Exception

**Hook:** Drail visits Bramblebridge to praise reform and dismiss local abuses as implementation
errors while quietly removing records.

**Layers:** stern reformer → architect of selective failure → subordinate of Vane's policy office,
with personal choices exceeding his orders.

**Objectives:**

1. Preserve a complete comparison of policy, enforcement, exemptions, and consequences.
2. Prevent Drail from sacrificing Fara, Rusk, or junior clerks as the whole explanation.
3. Secure an accountable outcome that leaves roads and trade functioning.

**Routes:** formal inquiry, clerk coalition, intercepted destruction, courtroom advocacy, political
bargain, public satire, direct confrontation, or evidence delivery to the Crown Council.

**Clues:** targets set before consultation; Drail's corrections in Fara's orders; favoured freight
contracts; Vane's legitimate concern about succession logistics; separate orders showing Drail added
coercive family threats himself.

**Failure forward:** Drail keeps office but loses secrecy; future travel faces surveillance and new
appeal opportunities.

**Aftermath:** the party's new travel authorization books a ship immobilized by a labour dispute.

### Q07 — The Comedy Crown

**Hook:** Bramble Theatre Loft loses its comic villain crown, which contains a ministerial dispatch
hidden by someone who mistook costume storage for a secure courier cache.

**Layers:** theatrical theft → competing actors and officials → dispatch discusses succession
contingencies and an unrelated flood appropriation.

**Objectives:**

1. Recover the crown before the evening performance or replace it convincingly.
2. Identify who hid and who stole the dispatch without exposing private theatre matters needlessly.
3. Decide where the dispatch belongs and what public-interest information cannot stay hidden.

**Routes:** backstage search, impersonation, prop reconstruction, market chase, negotiation with a
fan, legal delivery, memorization, or staging the theft as part of the performance.

**Clues:** crown lining stitched by Jory; glitter on Bridge House forms; two actors swapped roles;
the thief wanted the jewels, not the paper; Sera knows an official voice from rehearsal patronage.

**Failure forward:** the performance becomes a glorious disaster and the dispatch is partly read
aloud, creating political urgency but additional witnesses.

**Aftermath:** the troupe invites the party to Bellafont, opening the unrelated orchard story.

### Q08 — Three Bridges, Two Courts

**Hook:** Floodwater threatens Merecross while three bridge owners and two courts dispute who may
close roads, requisition boats, and cut a private embankment.

**Layers:** bureaucratic comedy → real class and jurisdiction conflict → old flood map deliberately
omitted a poor district.

**Objectives:**

1. Protect people and essential routes during rising water.
2. Establish which interventions are physically necessary and legally contestable.
3. Record emergency choices so temporary power does not become permanent silently.

**Routes:** engineering, evacuation, persuasion, commandeering with later accountability, alternate
channels, local knowledge, public notices, or magical assistance within accepted mechanics.

**Clues:** Nessa's notebooks; water marks behind paneling; omitted district on royal map; mill gate
timing; an estate wall built over emergency spill land.

**Failure forward:** property and one bridge are lost, but lives can still be saved and the aftermath
becomes a reform rather than a reset.

**Aftermath:** displaced Dunmarrow singers need help reaching a funeral competition.

### Q09 — The Heir in the Footnote

**Hook:** Highmead scholars find a marginal note suggesting Alderwick's succession law allows a
broader council choice than the Crown publicly admits.

**Layers:** secret rightful heir → recent annotation and older ambiguous custom → political actors
want certainty more than historical truth can provide.

**Objectives:**

1. Authenticate each layer of the manuscript and its cited precedents.
2. Hear living stakeholders whose rights cannot be reduced to the text.
3. Prevent one faction from presenting interpretation as automatic destiny.

**Routes:** palaeography, oral history, court debate, copy comparison, foreign archive request,
protecting scholars, or publishing uncertainty deliberately.

**Clues:** ink age mismatch; a missing witness roll; related Veyr custom; Queen Adela's private notes;
proof the annotation reflects a real but exceptional precedent.

**Failure forward:** one faction weaponizes the note, but opposing evidence and later negotiation
remain available.

**Aftermath:** a scholar pays with a deed to a house whose roof crosses an old property line.

## Volume III — Roads That Do Not Agree

### Q10 — The Ship That Must Sail

**Hook:** Gullstrand shipwrights strike for safe hours and injury support while the autumn grain
convoy deadline approaches and someone sabotages a fitting.

**Layers:** reckless strikers or greedy owners → planted sabotage by a safety inspector → procurement
broker and grain speculator seek to break the strike and profit from delay.

**Objectives:**

1. Keep the yard and unfinished ship safe while identifying the sabotage method.
2. Establish a workable plan for grain transport and legitimate worker demands.
3. Expose or neutralize the commercial network profiting from failure.

**Routes:** engineering inspection, negotiation, alternate vessel repair, financial trail, protect
apprentice witness, public test, magical repair if supported, or organizing shared oversight.

**Clues:** wrong-direction stress marks; inspector-only tool access; futures contracts; falsified
injury logs; timber from a protected Alderwick grove.

**Failure forward:** the convoy sails late or smaller, changing prices and relief needs but not
ending the campaign.

**Aftermath:** a safe ship carries Selucian wedding delegates with a separate seating crisis.

### Q11 — The Grey Orchard Wife

**Hook:** Bellafont's pale-canker kills old trees, and villagers blame the Grey Orchard Wife while
estates sell an expensive cure.

**Layers:** spirit curse → soil/water stress and infected grafts → lost common-water rights worsen
the disease; a real supernatural presence may preserve memory rather than cause blight.

**Objectives:**

1. Compare disease spread, water access, graft sources, and reported apparitions.
2. Stop harmful treatment and protect remaining healthy stock.
3. Resolve immediate water access while deciding how to treat the grove's spirit claim.

**Routes:** plant study, irrigation survey, witness folklore, night vigil, negotiation, graft
quarantine, archive recovery, or communication with a possible spirit.

**Clues:** disease follows graft batches not sightings; oldest trees fail first where canals were
redirected; orchard song contains boundary sequence; apparitions point away from sick trees.

**Riddle:** seven carved pears ask visitors to “give the first drink to the root that owns no fruit.”
The neglected common rootstock identifies the old shared spring. Two hints come from song order and
water stains.

**Failure forward:** more trees are cut, but surviving stock and legal testimony remain.

**Aftermath:** restored water exposes a grave whose memorial creates a quiet personal story.

### Q12 — The Shepherd Who Refused

**Hook:** Veyr succession factions all claim shepherd Tom as a symbolic supporter; Tom refuses the
office, while someone plans violence to make refusal impossible.

**Layers:** hidden wise king → capable mediator who genuinely does not want rule → rival campaigns
need legitimacy and one escort company needs instability.

**Objectives:**

1. Keep candidates, delegates, and Tom safe through the moot.
2. Expose manufactured violence and false-raid funding.
3. Help the marches reach a legitimate process without forcing a preferred candidate.

**Routes:** guard work, negotiation, public procedure, financial evidence, challenge contests,
decoy banners, Tom's local mediation, or delaying election lawfully.

**Clues:** raid tack from one company; payment through Vanner's cousin; Red's genuine emergency
plans; Tom's past settlements; contradictory heraldic blank thefts.

**Failure forward:** the election fractures or a weaker candidate wins, but local marches remain
active agents and later reform is possible.

**Aftermath:** a thank-you horse fears one harmless old waymark, opening a Road That Forgets quest.

### Q13 — The Choir Below Stone

**Hook:** Copperdeep miners hear formal knocking and choral tones below sealed Concord galleries.

**Layers:** trapped ghosts → ventilation, old bell pipes, and active trespass → someone searches for
a tithe chamber associated with dragon history.

**Objectives:**

1. Map safe access and protect workers from collapse or bad air.
2. Determine the source of knocking, soot, and choral resonance.
3. Decide what evidence and access should remain sealed, studied, or made public.

**Routes:** mining expertise, sound triangulation, old plans, rescue ropes, ventilation smoke,
controlled breach, magical sensing, or following trespass supply lines.

**Clues:** human boot soot; pipes tuned to warning intervals; fresh food scraps; tithe niche marks;
one knocking sequence is an old shift code.

**Failure forward:** a gallery collapse closes one route but exposes a safer archive escape tunnel.

**Aftermath:** bell metal carries a Kethrian tithe mark, unrelated to Alderwick tolls.

### Q14 — The Wrong Rescue Contract

**Hook:** The party and Wayward Company are each hired to find “Perrin Vale,” one a missing childless
merchant and the other a retired actor using the same stage name.

**Layers:** rival rescue race → two unrelated disappearances crossing the same road → each missing
person left voluntarily for a different urgent reason.

**Objectives:**

1. Separate the identities, timelines, and actual risks without wasting rescue time.
2. Locate both Perrins or establish safe contact.
3. Resolve competing contracts fairly and protect private motives that cause no harm.

**Routes:** cooperative task split, rivalry, road interviews, theatre records, merchant ledgers,
tracking, public notices, or staged message.

**Clues:** different boot sizes; same signature learned from playbill; two inns remember opposite
food preferences; one seeks a sibling, one avoids a forced business marriage.

**Failure forward:** one rescue succeeds first and the other person moves farther, but new contact
and clues remain.

**Aftermath:** both parties receive lodging at an inn whose guest book records tomorrow's arrival.

### Q15 — A Funeral in Good Voice

**Hook:** A Dunmarrow veterans' choir loses its conductor before a competitive memorial performance,
and one song contains testimony challenging an honoured officer's history.

**Layers:** petty contest sabotage → conductor withdrew over disputed verse → forged veteran claim
protects a living family's status.

**Objectives:**

1. Help the memorial proceed without treating grief as evidence theatre.
2. Authenticate the disputed verse and veteran claim through independent sources.
3. Choose a truthful form of public remembrance that does not punish innocent descendants.

**Routes:** music, interviews, archive work, battlefield travel, private mediation, alternate
performance, legal claim review, or allowing multiple verses.

**Clues:** metre difference dates verse; pension records; Lysa Norn's melody cipher; officer's
private letter; survivor accounts that disagree on motive but agree on event.

**Failure forward:** the contest becomes acrimonious, but the memorial and later truth process can
still occur.

**Aftermath:** a choir member asks help with a mundane but urgent leaking roof before winter.

## Volume IV — Across the Lantern Sea

### Q16 — A Wedding, If Convenient

**Hook:** A royal wedding could settle maritime claims, but Cook Berto vanishes, dock assemblies
threaten to stop work, and private treaty clauses transfer an island without its delegates.

**Layers:** comic wedding chaos → labour and representation dispute → advisers use ceremony to make
contested clauses politically irreversible.

**Objectives:**

1. Find Berto and keep essential hospitality functioning without forced labour.
2. Bring island and dock concerns into a process before vows make silence costly.
3. Protect the couple's agency while exposing, revising, delaying, or defeating hidden clauses.

**Routes:** cooking, negotiation, strike kitchen help, legal review, island delegation, public toast,
postponement, alternate venue, or private confrontation.

**Clues:** Berto's ingredient orders feed strikers; side agreement uses obsolete island name;
Princess Elia's annotations demand publication; advisers reroute delegate boats.

**Failure forward:** the wedding delays or proceeds amid protest, changing diplomacy without ending
the relationship or campaign.

**Aftermath:** the couple's saffron gift comes from a village absent from Namar records.

### Q17 — The Compass of an Unbuilt Ship

**Hook:** Tavaros returns a salt-worn compass engraved with the name of a ship scheduled to be built
next year.

**Layers:** prophecy → reused metal and future procurement fraud → the compass also reacts to one
older Concord route no modern chart records.

**Objectives:**

1. Authenticate materials, engraving sequence, wear, and ownership.
2. Determine how the future ship name entered the object and why Tavaros returned it now.
3. Follow or secure the hidden route without surrendering the compass to one claimant.

**Routes:** craft analysis, dock records, question dragon witnesses, sea trial, future contract
investigation, historical chart comparison, or controlled duplicate.

**Clues:** engraving newer than wear; brass from a decommissioned ship; Pella's mentor's tool marks;
needle deviation near a sealed Hall key; future contract secretly pre-awarded.

**Failure forward:** a claimant seizes the compass, but copies, craft evidence, and Tavaros's route
remain.

**Aftermath:** the procurement fraud is independent; the hidden route leads toward Aster Quay.

### Q18 — Three Resignation Letters

**Hook:** Missing League Chair Ansel Daro left three authentic resignation letters, each naming a
different reason, successor, and place of refuge.

**Layers:** indecisive politician → deliberate contingency against three anticipated kidnappers → a
fourth actor helped Ansel escape all of them.

**Objectives:**

1. Determine intended recipient and trigger for each letter.
2. Locate Ansel or establish voluntary safe contact.
3. Prevent factions from using contradictory letters to seize League process.

**Routes:** handwriting and seal analysis, courier interviews, public procedural challenge, follow
three false trails, decode grocery list, protect secret refuge, or stage a fourth resignation.

**Clues:** different paper watermarks; one impossible seasonal fruit; punctuation as harbour basin
numbers; dry-sock payment; Ansel's known habit of writing contingency apologies.

**Riddle:** “I leave by three doors and remain in the room. Which resignation is mine?” The answer is
the office, not Ansel: each letter resigns a different power he held. Hints come from titles and
missing personal pronouns.

**Failure forward:** one faction installs an acting chair, but Ansel and the League retain legal
paths to respond.

**Aftermath:** the impossible fruit points to stolen durable seed.

### Q19 — The Flowers Inside the Sealed Hall

**Hook:** Fresh flowers appear weekly inside the Concord hall sealed since Year 1 AS.

**Layers:** ghostly remembrance → hidden maintenance route and civic ritual → Council of Margins
uses the same route to inspect archive seals.

**Objectives:**

1. Observe or enter the Hall without damaging records or creating a public crush.
2. Identify the flower bearer, maintenance route, and separate later intruders.
3. Decide immediate custody and access while preserving evidence of Council activity.

**Routes:** stakeout, architecture, old workers' families, roof access, drainage tunnel, ritual
participation, legal petition, or magical sensing.

**Clues:** two kinds of mud; flowers from Brokers' Orchard; old municipal invoices; wax flecks under
archive door; bearer is an elderly cleaner preserving a private memorial, not the spy.

**Failure forward:** public attention forces the Hall open under poor conditions, increasing
political actors but also transparency.

**Aftermath:** archive evidence connects Celyn to the Council without explaining seed theft or
missing villages.

## Volume V — Names That Went Missing

### Q20 — The Village That Is Not There

**Hook:** The party follows saffron to a populated Namar village absent from tax rolls, maps, and
royal service lists.

**Layers:** smuggler settlement → community erased long ago for protection → current brokers exploit
legal invisibility and Sable Annek collects erased names.

**Objectives:**

1. Earn community consent and understand benefits and harms of invisibility.
2. Trace current grain, tax, service, and identity exploitation.
3. Pursue recognition, protected status, continued secrecy, or another negotiated form chosen with
   residents.

**Routes:** puppet performance, canal work, legal research, broker infiltration, royal petition,
mutual-aid records, secret census controlled by residents, or confronting collectors.

**Clues:** songs list villages; dry-sock accounts; cargo weights; old purge order; black-scale tokens;
families with births but no legal deaths.

**Failure forward:** officials discover the place before protection is ready, creating an urgent
defence and negotiation rather than erasing the village.

**Aftermath:** recognition festival needs a missing prop monster, a mostly unrelated comic quest.

### Q21 — Seed for the Wrong Season

**Hook:** Namar granaries contain short-lived seed while durable flood-resistant stock appears in
foreign warehouses.

**Layers:** theft by hungry farmers → substitution inside royal distribution → export network backed
by officials and investors betting on next year's shortage.

**Objectives:**

1. Test seed, storage conditions, and distribution records before planting.
2. Identify substitution points and protect whistleblowers.
3. Restore enough viable stock while choosing how to expose or disrupt the market scheme.

**Routes:** agricultural expertise, warehouse tracking, controlled purchase, canal interception,
public germination test, negotiation with Seed Mothers, or alternate stock exchange.

**Clues:** husk colour, storage dust, three resignation-letter grocery list, export seals, missing
barge time, one broker's unusually accurate weather insurance.

**Failure forward:** part of the crop fails, creating relief and political consequences but also
clear proof.

**Aftermath:** a copper payment in the export chain points to Kethria's separate tithe fraud.

**Failure forward:** Karsh wakes earlier and demands explanation, shifting the quest to high-stakes

**Aftermath:** recovered bell metal rings a Lornesse warning pattern, opening a rescue mystery.

## Volume VI — The Margins of Peace

**Layers:** secret ruling cabal → divided custodians, restorationists, and reformers → no member has

**Objectives:**

### Q27 — The Archive That Answers Back

**Hook:** Ilyr's Listening Caves echo visitors' words with contradictory historical testimony.

**Layers:** dragon treasure vault → archive designed to resist single narratives → Ilyr's selective
custody is itself a form of power.

**Objectives:**

1. navigate the caves while preserving group memory, safety, and consent.
2. obtain corroborated testimony about the Concord, Council, and dragon's role.
3. persuade, compel, bargain with, expose, rob, or oppose Ilyr regarding archive custody.

**Routes:** riddles, testimony comparison, respectful parley, stealth, public delegation, copy teams,
legal/moral argument, dragon combat only if supported and unavoidable.

**Clues:** echoes disagree consistently by witness position; missing testimonies leave salt patterns;
Ilyr's early advice; letters proving withdrawal; communities requesting records and receiving jokes.

**Riddle:** “What belongs to you, is spoken by others, changes when written, and dies when only one
keeper remains?” Acceptable answers include name, story, testimony, or reputation if argued from
the cave evidence. Hints: ask whose voice the echo uses; compare a living witness to a sealed text.

**Failure forward:** Ilyr limits access or scatters records, but the party leaves with partial
testimony and a public claim the dragon must answer.

**Aftermath:** evidence returns the campaign to Alderwick's unresolved succession and the coming
Accord congress.

### Q29 — The Accord Congress

**Hook:** Twelve polities and many nonstate institutions meet at Aster Quay to address counterfeit

**Layers:** diplomatic summit → each delegation brings a valid local fear → actors try to package

**Objectives:**

1. keep delegates, witnesses, couriers, and public access functioning through pressure and sabotage.
2. prevent one bloc from controlling evidence, procedure, or emergency powers.
3. help produce, reject, split, or replace an accord whose consequences are intelligible.

**Routes:** diplomacy, security, public testimony, procedural challenge, side agreements, strikes,
street theatre, archive evidence, magical verification where lawful, or walking away with coalition
partners.

**Clues:** every prior region supplies evidence; sabotage attempts have different sponsors; one
delegate is framed with an authentic but obsolete seal; Ilyr's testimony contradicts itself openly.

**Failure forward:** the congress collapses into regional agreements, which may be less elegant but
more consensual and still avert war.

**Aftermath:** the party must decide what practical system replaces the defeated or discredited
parts of the Seventh Ledger.

### Q30 — The Measure of Mercy

**Hook:** With conspirators exposed and archives opened, ordinary disputes still require records,
standards, routes, and enforcement. Destroying the old system is easier than replacing its useful
functions.

**Layers:** final boss victory → institutional vacuum → the campaign's true conclusion is how power,
memory, and correction are distributed.

**Objectives:**

1. identify essential services, vulnerable people, dangerous powers, and trustworthy local owners.
2. establish a bounded transition for records, standards, roads, debts, privacy, and correction.
3. return to affected communities and accept the visible costs and benefits of the chosen settlement.

**Routes:** distributed archives, independent standards guild, reformed Council, public copies with
privacy rules, royal compact, community custody, dragon testimony, deliberate plural systems, or
another evidence-backed design.

**Clues:** every prior consequence matters; no document supplies a perfect answer; NPC trust and
institutions earned during play determine what is feasible.

**Creative constraint:** the final solution cannot make all values compatible. It must state who can
decide, who can appeal, what remains secret, and what happens when records are wrong.

**Failure forward:** an imperfect arrangement still ends the campaign honestly; future adventures
can involve repair, misuse, and ordinary life under the new system.

**Aftermath:** meals, letters, repairs, memorials, comic local disputes, and changed relationships
show what “victory” means. The next campaign can begin with a completely unrelated coastal rescue,
inheritance problem, creature migration, or distant invitation.

## Quest-atlas review rules

- Essential conclusions have at least three clue routes across people, objects/places, records, or
  observed consequences.
- Wrong guesses can be tested; hidden truth is not rewritten merely to reward confidence.
- Apparent bosses can have higher sponsors without making the lower conflict irrelevant.
- At least one adventure in every volume is independent of the Seventh Ledger.
- Rewards and consequences remain proposals until their runtime owners exist.
- Every packet should be adaptable to character abilities and creative plans without assuming one
  required class, spell, NPC survival, combat, or successful roll.
