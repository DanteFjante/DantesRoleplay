# Caldris history and lore atlas — disputed pasts and living customs

Status: **review content; not authoritative chronology or player knowledge**
World eras: [Caldris World bible](CALDRIS-WORLD-BIBLE.md#the-seven-eras)
Locations: [Caldris expanded locations](CALDRIS-EXPANDED-LOCATIONS.md)
Slice: [Caldris Authoring Slice 2](CALDRIS-AUTHORING-SLICE-2-IMPLEMENTATION.md)

This atlas adds twenty-four historical incidents and twenty-four pieces of living lore. “Record”
means the current scholarly consensus inside the review setting, not a runtime fact. Every incident
contains a disagreement or missing piece so history can be investigated rather than merely recited.

## Twenty-four historical incidents

| Polity | Era and incident | Broadly accepted record | Contested or concealed layer | Present story consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alderwick | **First Footprints — The Winter Sharing** | Camps along the Mere exchanged smoked fish, flint, and shelter during a severe winter. | Later royal histories call one camp ancestral rulers, though burial goods suggest equal partners. | Competing museums claim the Button Hills storehouses; descendants want a shared memorial instead. |
| Alderwick | **Crownmaking — The Three-Day Queen** | Mara Vale ruled for three days, signed emergency flood orders, then yielded to her elder cousin. | Her abdication may have been conditional on permanent village appeal rights omitted from court copies. | River communities invoke “Mara's hearing” against modern toll reforms. |
| Dunmarrow | **Hearths and Standing Stones — The Road Away** | Highland communities built a stone route leading south from defensive ridges. | It may have been an organized evacuation from something below the fells, not a trade road. | Peat fire at Blackcap Fell exposes markers pointing toward sealed mines. |
| Dunmarrow | **Sundering — The Lanternless Watch** | Greyharrow held its walls for eleven nights after its signal oil ran out. | Moor villages supplied hidden lamps but were excluded from the military epic because several were rebel sympathizers. | Veterans and villagers argue over names on a new monument. |
| Carrowmere | **River Thrones — The Forty Oars** | Forty crews rescued a coastal settlement after an earthquake-driven wave. | Surviving oar marks include Selucian patterns, contradicting nationalist versions on both shores. | Brinewife's Steps reveals the shared compact and possible mutual harbour rights. |
| Carrowmere | **Long Reprieve — The Grain Fleet Refusal** | Shipwrights refused to launch unsafe grain vessels despite royal pressure during famine. | A court clerk quietly altered deadlines, preventing arrests, then vanished from records. | Current labour leaders seek the clerk's descendants while officials fear precedent. |
| Bellafont | **First Footprints — Seeds Beneath Ash** | Charred pear and quince seeds show people replanted orchards after a volcanic haze darkened summers. | The seeds came from several distant valleys, evidence of wider migration than court genealogies admit. | Orchard blight revives debate over sharing protected old rootstock. |
| Bellafont | **Lantern Concord — The Feast Without Knives** | Rival orchard lords negotiated irrigation rights at a feast where all knives were locked away. | Kitchen workers secretly kept tools and enforced the truce when nobles tried to leave. | Their descendants possess an overlooked right to witness water treaties. |
| Lornesse | **Hearths and Standing Stones — The Woven Silence** | Copper curtains were hung in mountain chambers for unknown ceremonies. | Tool marks suggest they were practical sound barriers protecting settlements from dangerous resonances. | Seven Echo Quarry has reopened one such chamber, challenging sacred and industrial claims. |
| Lornesse | **Crownmaking — The Widows' Descent** | Miners' families used freight sledges to evacuate Copperdeep during a tunnel fire. | Officials later credited a duke who arrived after the rescue and took ownership of the incline. | The cooperative now challenges royal control of the Widow's Incline. |
| Veyr Marches | **River Thrones — The Tenth Hearth** | Ten hill communities once shared the Ninepost market. | One community was erased from later charters after sheltering refugees deemed rebels. | The mysterious tenth post could restore land and voting claims to scattered families. |
| Veyr Marches | **Three Banner War — Supper at the Courteous Ruin** | Enemy patrols unknowingly shared shelter during a blizzard and left without fighting. | The caretaker deliberately seated officers by childhood nickname so they recognized kinship ties. | The surviving guest book could cool a feud—or embarrass heroic family legends. |
| Ordelain | **River Thrones — The Floating Court** | Judges heard river disputes from a movable platform so no bank controlled proceedings. | The platform was also designed to escape rulers who tried to seize the court. | Reed Judge's Rest drifting downstream creates a serious constitutional absurdity. |
| Ordelain | **Sundering — The Blank Decree** | A peace decree survives only as seals around a blank centre. | Reactive ink may have deliberately vanished after signatories fulfilled a temporary arrangement. | Modern factions invent convenient wording while Inkfish Wharf offers a way to recover impressions. |
| Namarra | **First Footprints — The Door Above Water** | A stone granary was built on high ground before the delta took its present shape. | Seed jars may contain crops from a cooler climate and evidence that the “first” delta settlers arrived later. | Drowned Door Orchard becomes important to both food security and identity. |
| Namarra | **Long Reprieve — The Villages That Declined Counting** | Several delta villages stopped appearing in tax rolls after a disastrous census. | Water captains hid them with quiet royal consent so emergency grain would not be seized for debt. | Their descendants face legal nonexistence when seeking aid or land protection. |
| Tessarane | **Hearths and Standing Stones — The Other Horizon** | Plateau builders tracked sunrise from a rotating stone terrace. | Its original horizon markers point toward a mountain now absent from maps and perhaps removed by ancient collapse. | Cloud Apricot Farm's moving terrace may reveal routes into a buried valley. |
| Tessarane | **Lantern Concord — The Patient Stair Compact** | Polities jointly funded high-pass steps to keep trade open. | Shepherd clans supplied most labour in exchange for toll exemptions later narrowed by scribes. | Road keepers and pastoral families challenge present toll collection. |
| Selucia | **River Thrones — The First Borrowed Bell** | Islanders raised a wrecked foreign bell as a storm warning. | They rescued its crew and received the bell freely, while the mainland record calls it salvage theft. | A return order at Borrowed Bell Isle threatens to reopen the story. |
| Selucia | **Crownmaking — The Palace Vote** | An unfinished palace became public land after architects failed to agree on a roof. | Measurements in its one completed room encode how island levies were meant to rotate. | A private ownership suit could unexpectedly change tax obligations across the kingdom. |
| Kethria | **Hearths and Standing Stones — The Mountain Portion** | Copper, bread, and woven cloth were carried to a high cleft each winter. | The deliveries may have begun as disaster relief to isolated miners before being reinterpreted as dragon tribute. | Modern tithe advocates and skeptics possess only half the moral history. |
| Kethria | **Three Banner War — The Empty Saddles** | A winter ambush killed an entire caravan, memorialized by saddles at the pass. | Several members survived, deserted the war, and built lives under new names. | Returning saddles may expose families whose peace depends on an honorable lie. |
| Seven Lamps League | **Lantern Concord — The Fifth Store** | A mobile warehouse let neutral supplies pass blockades during the Concord's founding crisis. | Merchants later preserved its exemption for private profit long after the emergency. | The Fifth Warehouse is both a peace relic and a current customs fraud. |
| Seven Lamps League | **Long Reprieve — The Meal Between Minutes** | A deadlocked council resumed after delegates shared lunch and rewrote a shipping compromise. | Later chairmen removed the meal-pause clause to shorten testimony from distant communities. | Chairman's Nap can restore the forgotten procedural protection. |

## Twenty-four living lore, customs, and disputed beliefs

| Polity | Lore or custom | Everyday practice | What people disagree about | Story use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alderwick | **The Last Button Home** | Travelers leave one spare button at a hill cairn and collect a different one on returning. | Some call it luck; historians think it once counted families using famine stores. | A button links a missing traveler to a route they publicly denied taking. |
| Alderwick | **Thirteen Is an Honest Bell** | A thirteenth strike means “the count is wrong; come verify it,” never bad luck. | Bridge chapters disagree whether any citizen may ring it. | A comic civic rule becomes a reliable alarm and a source of jurisdictional conflict. |
| Dunmarrow | **A Rook Owes One Blow** | Smiths pause after a rook perfectly copies a hammer rhythm. | Miners say it remembers the dead; smiths say the pause prevents fatigue. | A rook repeats a rhythm used only in a supposedly sealed workshop. |
| Dunmarrow | **Crutch Courtesy** | At mountain inns, the least mobile guest chooses the first seat and safest departure time. | Escort captains treat it as quaint; locals treat it as survival law. | Invoking courtesy can defeat a dangerous monopoly without drawing steel. |
| Carrowmere | **Blue Roof Reckoning** | Families paint a rescued tile with the name of the wreck it came from. | Some names belong to smuggling craft officially declared never to exist. | A roof becomes an accidental archive of maritime secrets. |
| Carrowmere | **The Dry Admiral's Toast** | Retired captains toast a voyage that never occurred, adding one implausible detail each year. | One “joke” detail consistently matches covert wartime charts. | Players must separate affectionate nonsense from encoded testimony. |
| Bellafont | **Rain Letters** | Orchard workers leave anonymous encouragement in preserve jars at roadside shelters. | Court poets claim to have invented it; workers insist the custom is older. | A threatening message violates the form in ways that reveal its writer. |
| Bellafont | **The Blunt Dessert** | Difficult negotiations end with an intentionally ugly but delicious tart. | Nobles debate its symbolism; cooks say it prevents people pretending appearance is substance. | Refusing or praising it communicates more than formal speeches. |
| Lornesse | **Goat Judgment** | Grazing disputes are argued before an elected “goat judge,” who announces a practical compromise. | Outsiders think the goat decides; locals know the decorated animal keeps tempers down. | An antagonist dismisses the ritual and loses community trust before a serious hearing. |
| Lornesse | **The Missing Echo** | Workers stop immediately if a familiar echo fails to return. | Priests call it mountain displeasure; engineers call it changing voids. | Both interpretations point toward real danger through different evidence. |
| Veyr Marches | **Tenth-Plate Supper** | Nine guests set a tenth plate for an absent enemy, exile, or relative. | Some marches honor reconciliation; others say it commemorates betrayal. | The named absence reveals how a household remembers a political conflict. |
| Veyr Marches | **Axle Truth** | A host may ask one blunt question while helping repair a traveler's wheel. | Free Axle companies argue whether answering is obligatory. | The custom creates intimate conversation without magical truth enforcement. |
| Ordelain | **Curved Boundaries Are Wiser** | Farmers preserve bends in hedges and paths even after rivers move. | Lawyers call it superstition; flood workers recognize lost channels. | A “nonsensical” boundary becomes a life-saving drainage map. |
| Ordelain | **Black-Pawed Testimony** | An ink-stained cat crossing a courtroom pauses proceedings for water. | Judges insist it is hygiene, not luck; everyone still watches which party the cat approaches. | A light comic beat exposes tampered ink on a litigant's sleeve. |
| Namarra | **Three Spoons, No Fourth** | Communal kitchens use three serving spoons for children, workers, and guests. | Reformers want a fourth for officials; cooks consider officials guests. | A seating dispute reveals who expects privilege during crisis. |
| Namarra | **Paper Boats Carry Small Truths** | Children float unsigned worries to a trusted downstream elder. | Adults debate whether reading them violates privacy or fulfills the custom. | A paper fleet contains unrelated fears plus one crucial warning. |
| Tessarane | **The Patient Step** | A traveler who needs rest places a blue cord on the stair; others pass without comment and may leave water. | Court pilgrims romanticize what began as an accessibility practice. | Respecting the custom earns trust; counterfeit cords hide a courier route. |
| Tessarane | **Kites Do Not Lie, Knots Do** | Weather kites are trusted, but their cord knots are treated as human opinion. | Scholars reverse the proverb when discussing measurements. | Conflicting signals force players to inspect both nature and interpretation. |
| Selucia | **Every Bell Was Borrowed** | Bell owners recite where each bell came from before storms. | Some accounts are gifts, some salvage, and some polite theft. | Provenance becomes an emotional mystery rather than a simple ownership check. |
| Selucia | **One Roofless Wedding Wish** | Couples at the Unfinished Palace make one promise that cannot depend on weather. | Priests debate whether it is sacred; sailors say it is merely sensible. | A disrupted wedding still matters because the central promise remains possible. |
| Kethria | **The Shield Loaf** | A household breaks its stamped winter loaf only after confirming neighbors also have bread. | Wealthy homes treat it symbolically; mountain villages still conduct the check. | Missing flour becomes a network of mutual aid rather than a solitary theft. |
| Kethria | **The Unridden Saddle** | One saddle remains empty on dangerous crossings so the dead or lost are not forgotten. | Some survivors consider it comforting; others see a lie that prevents searches. | Removing a saddle can be grief, evidence, or protection. |
| Seven Lamps League | **A Fifth Price Exists** | Bargainers name four prices—coin, time, risk, and reputation—then ask what cost was omitted. | Merchants enjoy the saying; labourers answer “the person doing the work.” | The custom exposes hidden costs in apparently generous contracts. |
| Seven Lamps League | **Lunch Suspends Certainty** | During shared council meals, no statement may be entered as final minutes. | Powerful delegates call it inefficient; smaller towns rely on the candid interval. | Informal kindness produces evidence or compromise that formal debate could not. |

## History and lore use rules

- Present records through archives, monuments, songs, tools, landscapes, testimony, and habits—not
  as omniscient narration.
- Give important conclusions multiple routes: physical evidence, independent witnesses, practical
  consequences, and comparison between records.
- A disputed account need not conceal a villain. Memory changes through pride, grief, class,
  translation, administrative convenience, and sincere misunderstanding.
- Recovering truth should create choices about repair, recognition, safety, law, and belonging;
  publication is not automatically the kindest or wisest outcome.
- These entries are GM review material. Runtime dates, facts, rumours, secrets, clues, and player
  knowledge require their confirmed owners.
