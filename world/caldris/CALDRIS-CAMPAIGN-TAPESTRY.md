## Campaign promise

**The Measure of Mercy** is a level 1–10 story about how people make large systems humane—or fail
to—while trying to preserve ordinary lives worth living. It begins with a bridge toll and a bell
that rings thirteen times. It grows through regional corruption, royal succession, transcontinental
archives, and the historical responsibility of a dragon. Between those layers are independent
adventures about work, weather, creatures, weddings, food, art, rescue, land, grief, and friendship.

The party should repeatedly experience three satisfying kinds of transition:

1. **Higher layer:** defeating an apparent boss reveals a superior, sponsor, institution, or older
   cause. The previous victory still improves lives and changes the higher layer's options.
2. **Sideways consequence:** a solution helps one group and creates a new choice for another without
   implying hidden villainy.
3. **Fresh door:** an adventure concludes cleanly, then travel, celebration, reward, relationship,
   weather, work, or coincidence introduces a genuinely unrelated next problem.

## Four enduring threads

### Hearth — Bramblebridge and the places worth returning to

- **Visible question:** can the party become part of a community without taking over its life?
- **Pressure:** toll abuse, floods, work, small debts, strained institutions, and the changes caused
  by adventurers becoming locally important.
- **Recurring cast:** Elowen Pike, Nessa Quill, Tibb Fallow, Merrit Vale, Della Crookshaw, Brother
  Odo, Fenna Dorr, Sera Lark, Jory Fenner.
- **Escalation if ignored:** traders bypass town, the Kettle loses custom, mill debts rise, drainage
  work worsens floods, and Rusk's network becomes the only practical transport system.
- **Possible endings:** a fair local freight charter; a community-run bridge house; Crown control;
  tolerated smuggling; or a hybrid system the party helps negotiate.

### Crown — authority, succession, and the price of preventive control

- **Visible question:** who should hold emergency power when lawful succession is uncertain?
- **Pressure:** Queen Adela's undeclared heir, Chancellor Vane's centralization, provincial
  resentment, and rival interpretations of law.
- **Recurring cast:** Queen Adela, Vane, Provost Drail, Crown councillors, Highmead scholars, foreign
  diplomats, Veyr succession figures.
- **Escalation if ignored:** toll systems centralize, couriers become politicized, claimants seek
  foreign promises, and fear of war becomes justification for coercion.
- **Possible endings:** declared heir; reformed succession law; regency compact; elective council;
  divided crown powers; or a crisis that the party helps keep peaceful without deciding who rules.

### Road — independent adventures and cross-continent relationships

- **Visible question:** what obligations arise simply because the party passes through?
- **Pressure:** shipyards, orchards, rescue roads, festivals, lost messages, creature habitats,
  marriages, labour, local law, and people who need help for reasons unrelated to the main plot.
- **Recurring cast:** Wayward Company, Roadmenders, couriers, shipwrights, cooks, ferrymasters,
  performers, scholars, and local hosts.
- **Escalation if ignored:** problems resolve without the party, sometimes well and sometimes badly;
  later visits reflect that history.
- **Possible endings:** a network of trusted roads and hosts; a famous but complicated reputation;
  local resentments; or a decision to settle and let some distant calls pass to others.

### Deep history — who owns memory and obligation

- **Visible question:** why are destroyed Concord seals returning?
- **Pressure:** the Seventh Ledger, missing archives, false records, erased communities, dragons'
  historical roles, and the temptation to restore efficient systems without consent.
- **Recurring cast:** Celyn Marr, Council of Margins, Pella Sorn, archivists, missing League Chair
  Ansel Daro, Ilyr Vaust, Sable Annek, community record keepers.
- **Escalation if ignored:** institutions obey old forms automatically, people disappear legally,
  emergency powers normalize, and archive holders bargain separately with states.
- **Possible endings:** destroy central control while preserving open standards; distribute archive
  custody; reform the Council; expose every record at great risk; entrust selected keepers; or let a
  new compact arise from negotiated local rights.

## Six-volume campaign shape

### Volume I — Thirteen Bells (levels 1–2)

The party reaches or lives in Bramblebridge when the noon bell rings thirteen times and an empty tax
wagon rolls into market. Four local adventures establish the town, its humour, and material stakes.

1. **The Thirteenth Bell:** find the driver, explain the bell, and prevent panic or exploitation.
2. **Chickens of Commercial Intent:** challenge absurd toll enforcement and discover selective
   exemptions.
3. **The Wheel at Midnight:** investigate the mill stoppage, deed problem, and hidden sluice route.
4. **The Respectable Smuggler:** confront Rusk Pettifer and decide what lawful reform must replace
   the services his network actually provided.

Apparent completion: Rusk's network is exposed, bargained down, displaced, or turned witness.
Higher reveal: Rusk holds written instructions from Provost Drail. Fresh unrelated door: a rescued
tax-wagon horse carries Bellafont orchard fungus in its tack, inviting a later blight story that is
not caused by Drail.

### Volume II — The Price of a Fair Road (levels 2–4)

The party travels to Merecross and Highmead through flood season. Drail appears to be the organizer
of the toll scheme; court and civic stories reveal the succession problem without making the party
royal kingmakers immediately.

1. **A Standard with a Hollow Heart:** the hidden debt list in the guild weight names favoured
   carriers and one innocent family.
2. **The Provost's Exception:** prove how Drail made local failure support central reform.
3. **The Comedy Crown:** recover a prop crown and dispatch without destroying a theatre company.
4. **Three Bridges, Two Courts:** keep flood response working while institutions fight jurisdiction.
5. **The Heir in the Footnote:** uncover a recent legal interpretation without deciding succession
   by document alone.

Apparent completion: Drail loses office, reforms, bargains, flees, or testifies. Higher reveal:
Chancellor Vane authorized the policy model but not every abuse. Fresh unrelated door: the flood
damages a Roadmenders' inn whose repair fund was invested in a missing Carrowmere ship.

### Volume III — Roads That Do Not Agree (levels 4–6)

The party ranges across Eredane. Each region has its own problem; only some touch the Crown or
Seventh Ledger. This volume prevents conspiracy fatigue and builds continental relationships.

1. **The Ship That Must Sail:** navigate the justified Gullstrand strike, planted sabotage, and
   looming grain deadline.
2. **The Grey Orchard Wife:** address pale-canker, lost water rights, and a spirit story that may
   preserve legal memory.
3. **The Shepherd Who Refused:** protect a Veyr election from violence without forcing the best
   mediator to accept power.
4. **The Choir Below Stone:** explore Copperdeep's sealed levels and choose what evidence to surface.
5. **The Wrong Rescue Contract:** cooperate or compete with the Wayward Company after both parties
   are hired for different missing travellers with the same name.
6. **A Funeral in Good Voice:** honour war dead while exposing a forged veteran claim hidden in a
   choir competition.

Higher main-thread reveal: Vane uses Seventh Ledger analyses supplied by Celyn Marr. Independent
completion: ship safety, orchard water rights, and Veyr succession remain valuable whether or not
Celyn is confronted. Fresh door: a Selucian wedding invitation is offered as thanks for a rescue.

### Volume IV — Across the Lantern Sea (levels 6–7)

Travel to Solasca expands the World before the deepest plot becomes dominant. The journey includes
shipboard comedy, genuine danger, and several unrelated social obligations.

1. **A Wedding, If Convenient:** find Cook Berto, resolve labour obligations, and decide whether
   hidden treaty clauses should become public before the ceremony.
2. **The Compass of an Unbuilt Ship:** investigate Tavaros's returned compass and its future date.
3. **Three Resignation Letters:** find missing League Chair Ansel Daro and determine which anticipated
   kidnapper received which letter.
4. **The Flowers Inside the Sealed Hall:** enter or observe the former Concord chamber without
   surrendering its archive to one state.
5. **The Dry Sock Accounts:** protect mutual-aid records that accidentally prove a courier network.

Apparent completion: Celyn's Aster Quay cell is dismantled or divided. Higher reveal: Celyn answers
to the Council of Margins, whose factions disagree. Fresh unrelated door: the wedding couple's gift
of delta saffron arrives from a village that legally does not exist.

### Volume V — Names That Went Missing (levels 7–9)

The party moves through Namarra, Kethria, Tessarane, and Ordelain. Deep-history clues accumulate,
but each polity's crisis retains independent stakes.

1. **The Village That Is Not There:** restore, conceal, or transform the legal status of an erased
   community without exposing it to predatory collection.
2. **Seed for the Wrong Season:** trace stolen durable seed through royal and private granaries.
3. **The Doubled Tithe:** expose the fraudulent copper demand and confront the reality of Kethria's
   dragon bargain.
4. **The Ordinary Comet:** protect honest observation from three politically useful prophecies, then
   discover one genuinely impossible detail.
5. **The River Returns:** mediate claims around Old Aur Island and recover records older than the
   Concord.
6. **The Reed Graves:** meet or outwit Sable Annek, who hoards legal absences rather than gold.

Apparent completion: the Council's restoration plan becomes visible and several cells can be
defeated. Higher reveal: members invoke Ilyr Vaust's authority using an authentic old mark. Fresh
unrelated door: a surviving village asks the party to help stage a puppet festival celebrating its
return, and a prop monster goes missing for completely mundane reasons—at first.

### Volume VI — The Margins of Peace (levels 9–10)

The final volume is a struggle over institutions, history, and custody rather than a march toward
one required battle. The party can seek Ilyr, convene witnesses, expose records, bargain with
states, split the Council, or build a replacement practice from relationships earned throughout the
campaign.

1. **The Council of Margins:** reach, infiltrate, expose, debate, or fracture the transcontinental
   leadership.
2. **The Archive That Answers Back:** enter Ilyr's Listening Caves, where testimony contradicts
   official history and echo riddles test whether visitors listen rather than merely collect.
3. **The Queen's Empty Chair:** resolve Alderwick's succession pressure without allowing emergency
   policy to decide it silently.
4. **The Accord Congress:** keep delegates present and safe while each tries to control the archival
   settlement.
5. **The Measure of Mercy:** choose who may preserve, access, correct, and enforce shared records,
   and accept the imperfection of any answer.

The campaign closes with returns to affected communities, not only a summit. Bramblebridge's bridge,
the Gilded Kettle, the mill, the fen, the shipyards, orchards, villages, and NPC relationships show
what the party's grand decisions mean in ordinary life.

## Apparent-boss ladders

### Ladder A — the toll conspiracy

```text
Fara Dint, visible enforcer (coerced official, not true boss)
  → Rusk Pettifer, local organizer and Volume I apparent boss
    → Provost Halwen Drail, regional policy sponsor and Volume II apparent boss
      → Chancellor Sera Vane, national authority using the policy to prevent succession crisis
        → Auditor Celyn Marr, Seventh Ledger adviser shaping Vane's evidence
          → Council of Margins, distributed transcontinental leadership
            → Ilyr Vaust's historical responsibility and falsely invoked authority
```

Every step can end through exposure, reform, removal, bargaining, defection, legal defeat, flight,
or violence as fiction and supported mechanics permit. A higher reveal never restores a defeated
lower actor's control automatically.

### Ladder B — the shipyard sabotage

```text
frightened apprentice caught damaging a fitting
  → private safety inspector paying for staged evidence
    → royal procurement broker trying to break the strike
      → grain speculator expecting delayed convoys and higher prices
```

This ladder is independent of the Seventh Ledger. The “higher boss” is commercial leverage and a
small network of respectable interests. The strike can end through negotiated safety, alternate
shipping, public proof, worker control, or coercion with lasting consequences.

### Ladder C — the missing villages

```text
Emon Saar, boastful shell merchant who looks guilty
  → tax flotilla officers moving unrecorded grain
    → canal officials who erased villages to protect them during an old purge
      → current brokers exploiting that invisibility
        → Sable Annek, purchasing names and legal absence for a dragon archive
```

The apparent criminals include protectors, exploiters, and people trapped between. Restoring every
name publicly may expose households to debt; preserving invisibility may deny rights and services.

### Ladder D — the doubled dragon tithe

```text
warehouse thieves
  → Registrar Toma Var's forged authorization
    → one ambitious Keeper of the Mountain Tithe
      → foreign investors seeking to wake Karsh and disrupt Kethrian copper
        → Karsh Veyru, a real dragon whose bargain is not what the investors assume
```

The dragon is more dangerous than the human bosses but not necessarily the moral villain. The party
may preserve, renegotiate, publicize, or end the bargain.

## Consequence web

Choices should alter later conditions without prescribing one canonical morality.

- **Lawful reform without practical replacement** reduces corruption but may strand small traders.
- **Tolerated smuggling** preserves access but strengthens people who profit from secrecy.
- **Open archives** empower claimants and also expose vulnerable families.
- **Restricted archives** protect privacy but preserve keeper power.
- **Central coordination** can prevent war and enable coercion.
- **Local autonomy** preserves consent and may fail during cross-border emergencies.
- **Dragon bargains** can protect communities while making future generations pay for choices they
  did not make.
- **Public magic** can save lives and change how institutions treat the party thereafter.

The GM should track who benefited, who paid, what became public, which relationship changed, and
what practical service now exists or has vanished.

## Cozy return structure

After every two or three high-pressure adventures, return to or establish a warmth anchor:

- supper and changed gossip at the Gilded Kettle;
- flood preparation with Nessa in Candlefen;
- a terrible rehearsal at Bramble Theatre Loft;
- worker breakfasts in Copperdeep or Gullstrand;
- a festival, memorial meal, repair day, orchard pressing, or rescue-knot ceremony;
- correspondence from NPCs whose lives continued offscreen; and
- small consequences such as a repaired stair, renamed dish, new apprentice, changed sign, missing
  chair, or joke that only makes sense because of shared history.

## Tapestry boundary

This campaign shape can guide authored adventures and narration now. Durable parallel arcs,
branching chapters, general consequences, rewards, NPC disposition, chronology, and live quest packs
remain governed by the feature backlog and repository gates.
