Status: **eighteen review-content quest packets; not runtime quest records**

Every packet has exactly three initial objectives. Methods, discoveries, complications, and endings
remain open. “Thread” is an editorial suggestion; it does not place a quest into runtime state.

## Eredane roads and older ground

### Q31 — The Ford That Grew Too Deep

**Thread:** Hearth with an optional Deep-history door.

**Hook:** Sourapple Ford's autumn cattle drove arrives to find the crossing chest-deep, while the
upstream mill insists its repaired weir changed nothing.

**Layers:** blocked livestock road → competing water needs and an inaccurate repair survey →
face-down Concord stones beneath the old ford reveal someone redirected the channel decades ago.

**Objectives:**

1. Keep the herd, travelers, and downstream gardens safe while the crossing is unusable.
2. Establish what changed the water and which users have legitimate immediate needs.
3. Secure a workable crossing or water agreement before weather traps the drove.

**Approaches and clues:** Hesta reads reeds and anxious cattle; Nessa can compare ferry depth; mill
timbers show several generations of alteration; diving, temporary rope lanes, an upstream bypass,
court mediation, or coordinated gate timing can all work. Three stones align only when viewed from
the old riverbank.

**Failure forward:** Cattle scatter into orchards and tempers harden, but hoof tracks expose the old
road and force officials to provide emergency fodder.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** The drive ends with cider and exhausted singing. A button found in a
stone socket belongs to someone missing near the Button Hills, opening Q32 without implying the ford
was staged.

### Q32 — The Hills Keep Their Buttons

**Thread:** Deep history; independent of the Measure conspiracy.

**Hook:** A sinkhole swallows a shepherd's hut during steady rain, revealing shelves, sealed jars,
and hundreds of sorted buttons beneath a supposedly ancient tomb.

**Layers:** rescue in unstable chalk → pre-kingdom famine storehouse → a modern land claimant has
been removing seals to support an exclusive ancestral story.

**Objectives:**

1. Rescue anyone trapped and prevent a wider collapse around the opened chamber.
2. Determine the site's practical purpose without destroying its fragile evidence.
3. Decide how access, recovered supplies, and public interpretation should be shared.

**Approaches and clues:** Linnet's survey diagrams, children's button collections, airflow through
adjacent chambers, jar residue, nonhuman chew marks, and several burial traditions provide distinct
routes. The old inventory puzzle sorts objects by “what closes, what carries, what remembers”; a
button, jar lid, and marked tally belong together.

**Failure forward:** A chamber collapses before full recording, but displaced chalk outlines a
second entrance and surviving community tallies still disprove the exclusive claim.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** A recovered seed pouch is sent to Bellafont for identification. Its
courier instead reports trees flowering at the wrong time, offering Q37.

### Q33 — Water Owed to the Dead

**Thread:** Independent labour-and-memory story.

**Hook:** Latchwater's upper gate opens for a fourth night, draining the reservoir as an underground
peat fire approaches remote homes.

**Layers:** apparent sabotage → water exposes a sealed mine shaft → Marta's late colleague arranged
the releases so forgotten workers could finally be recovered and named.

**Objectives:**

1. Preserve enough water for fire control, households, and mill power.
2. Identify the gate operator and assess the sealed shaft before another release.
3. Find a lawful and physically safe way to address the buried workers.

**Approaches and clues:** Gate-handle wear, silt levels, old shift songs, union rolls, mine maps,
controlled pumping, a public vigil, or confronting the mine company all help. The night operator
will surrender if offered a credible recovery plan.

**Failure forward:** The reservoir drops below milling level, causing food and wage pressure, but
the exposed shaft becomes undeniable and emergency funds can be redirected toward both needs.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** During the memorial, a rook copies a hammer pattern last used at Rook
Anvil, where dangerous tools are already circulating—Q34.

### Q34 — One Blow Owed

**Thread:** Road story with a possible Concord-material clue.

**Hook:** A quarry chisel shatters during a public demonstration; moments later the rooks above Rook
Anvil repeat a hammer rhythm from an empty workshop.

**Layers:** flawed workmanship accusation → contaminated scrap supply → a broker deliberately filed
marks from salvaged Concord metal to avoid ownership restrictions.

**Objectives:**

1. Prevent further use of brittle tools without destroying innocent smiths' livelihoods.
2. Trace the metal from fracture patterns, accounts, transport, or witness memory.
3. Recover or contain the remaining scrap and decide how responsibility is assigned.

**Approaches and clues:** Ylsa can compare grain and break lines; Pella recognizes a repair mark;
rooks repeat the broker's loading rhythm; charcoal ash differs by valley. Public recall, discreet
replacement, buyer coalition, or trapping the broker with marked scrap are all viable.

**Failure forward:** Some stock leaves the district, but warning other craftspeople turns later
breakages into leads instead of surprise injuries.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** One buyer is the Dry Admiral's timber yard. The metal case can close
completely while the receipt introduces an unrelated theft in Q36.

## Coasts, orchards, and mountains

### Q35 — Forty Oars, Forty Names

**Thread:** Road and shared history.

**Hook:** Storm damage at Brinewife's Steps exposes an inscribed rescue compact just as Selucian
sailors request shelter under the rights it appears to grant.

**Layers:** emergency salt-pan repair → disputed translation and nationalist embarrassment → the
compact names ordinary crews omitted from both kingdoms' monuments.

**Objectives:**

1. Protect workers, sailors, and the salt terraces from the next tide.
2. Document enough of the compact to test the immediate harbour claim.
3. Negotiate shelter and future study without letting either crown seize the stone.

**Approaches and clues:** Tide knowledge, salt-worker oral history, Selucian bell patterns, charcoal
rubbings, photography-like magic only if accepted later, or a temporary co-custody agreement can
help. Missing name fragments repeat on blue roof tiles at Mackerel Crown.

**Failure forward:** The inscription suffers damage, but independent tile records and survivor songs
preserve its essential shared-rescue meaning.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** A celebratory shared meal is interrupted by two harbour speakers
presenting equally valid seals—an optional political episode unrelated to the compact's exposure.

### Q36 — The Dry Admiral's Fleet

**Thread:** Independent comic caper with real labour stakes.

**Hook:** Three enormous seasoned masts disappear from an inland yard without wheel tracks, and six
retired captains each claim to have predicted exactly this crime.

**Layers:** impossible theft → hidden flood channel and night flotation → the thieves are unpaid
yard workers selling one mast to cover injuries while a professional crew steals the other two.

**Objectives:**

1. Determine how the masts left and where the channel can carry them.
2. Separate the workers' grievance from the organized theft before blame becomes collective.
3. Recover the timber or its value while securing immediate safety for injured households.

**Approaches and clues:** Damp bark, reed cuts, moon tides, Tomas's excellent accounts, absurdly
embroidered captain stories, decoy timber, river pursuit, or bargaining with buyers all work. One
retiree's “imaginary” voyage accurately maps the flood channel.

**Failure forward:** One mast reaches the coast, but its distinctive growth rings identify the ship
using it and the yard gains time to settle wages.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** A grateful worker sends the party to a quiet cloth fair for rest. The
stolen ceremonial apron there opens a lighter unrelated mystery.

### Q37 — The Orchard Flowers Twice

**Thread:** Independent ecology and old infrastructure.

**Hook:** Quince Ladder blossoms a second time in late summer, beautiful enough to draw crowds and
dangerous enough to leave the trees too exhausted for winter.

**Layers:** suspected enchantment → soil warming along one terrace → a River Thrones conduit reopened
after an upstream excavation diverted mineral water.

**Objectives:**

1. Protect the orchard from trampling, fire risk, and panicked cutting.
2. Locate the heat source and determine whether the trees can recover.
3. Redirect or close the conduit while settling water claims created by its discovery.

**Approaches and clues:** Sabine's mule routes, root temperatures, moth behavior, old irrigation
verse, controlled trenching, grafting, water-court petition, or a temporary shared nursery can all
contribute. The verse's “winter river” means warm underground water, not a magical season.

**Failure forward:** Part of the orchard is lost, but cuttings preserve its strain and the new warm
water can support communal winter beds if governed fairly.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** Glass tubes used to inspect the conduit attract a strange moth swarm at
Mothglass House, a new problem that may be ecological or illicit but is not the same sabotage.

### Q38 — The Echo That Stayed

**Thread:** Deep history with a contained supernatural ambiguity.

**Hook:** Seven Echo Quarry loses one familiar echo; then a worker's whispered warning returns hours
later from inside solid stone.

**Layers:** unstable quarry acoustics → sound-absorbing copper chamber → old curtains stored and
released voices under particular wind and tension rather than housing a speaking ghost.

**Objectives:**

1. Find two missing workers and mark safe routes without relying on normal call-and-response.
2. Establish what the chamber does and whether blasting can continue.
3. Resolve sacred, scholarly, and livelihood claims before anyone removes the copper curtains.

**Approaches and clues:** Iven's cord system, touch signals, dust flow, warm breath, vibration,
written quarry records, or a negotiated quiet day all matter. A three-tone riddle—“first made,
second carried, third returned”—asks for blow, air, and echo.

**Failure forward:** The chamber is damaged, releasing a confusing chorus, but the missing workers
can follow vibrations out and enough curtain survives for study.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** One recorded voice names a person thought lost in the Three Banner War,
leading toward Kethria's Empty Saddle only if the table wants a sadder historical thread.

### Q39 — The Tenth Post

**Thread:** Crown and regional politics, not the Measure hierarchy.

**Hook:** A tenth carved post appears at Ninepost Field on the morning of a major moot, weathered as
if it has stood there for centuries.

**Layers:** provocative forgery → genuinely old post stored in a barn for two generations → heirs of
an erased march timed its return because the High Warden is dying.

**Objectives:**

1. Prevent the moot from destroying or seizing the post before examination.
2. Test its age, legal context, and the claimants' connection through multiple sources.
3. Secure a temporary voice, hearing, or settlement that does not prejudge every land claim.

**Approaches and clues:** Edrin reads punctuation; tool marks match the Courteous Ruin; Tenth-Plate
custom identifies scattered households; soil on the base proves recent placement but not forgery.
Formal injunction, public storytelling, guarded evidence, or bargaining among marches can work.

**Failure forward:** The post is split, exposing an internal maker's tally that preserves the claim
while making the symbol harder to weaponize.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** A claimant's wagon fails on a sabotaged road, naturally offering Q40's
journey south or a separate Free Axle investigation.

## Solascan rivers, skies, and islands

### Q40 — The Court That Would Not Stay Put

**Thread:** Road and institutional comedy with serious land consequences.

**Hook:** Reed Judge's Rest detaches during a hearing and drifts downstream with the judge, parties,
records, lunch, and one increasingly pleased courtroom cat.

**Layers:** river emergency → ancient detachable platform working as designed → rival banks attempt
to manipulate where the court lands so jurisdiction favours them.

**Objectives:**

1. Keep passengers and archives safe while controlling or accompanying the drifting court.
2. Determine why it detached and which landing choices are physically possible.
3. Preserve a fair hearing despite competing jurisdiction claims.

**Approaches and clues:** Boats, tow ropes, upstream anchors, old platform diagrams, Judge Miren's
precedent, negotiated neutral mooring, or continuing testimony afloat all work. The cat repeatedly
returns to the only dry compartment containing emergency steering poles.

**Failure forward:** Some records get wet and the platform grounds in disputed mud, but public
witnesses make a secret jurisdiction grab impossible.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** Replacement ink from Inkfish Wharf fades overnight, opening Q41 after
the court adventure has genuinely concluded.

### Q41 — A Decree in Vanishing Ink

**Thread:** Crown mystery with a higher sponsor, separate from Celyn Marr.

**Hook:** Testimony, debts, and a navigation decree fade from fresh official copies while private
duplicates remain clear.

**Layers:** defective ink → deliberate reactive lampblack substitution → a respected river-law
advocate commissioned it to erase evidence against a patron, who serves a coalition resisting
boundary reform.

**Objectives:**

1. Preserve or reconstruct the endangered texts before deadlines pass.
2. Trace the altered material through makers, couriers, and purchasing authority.
3. Stop the erasure scheme and protect witnesses without treating every ink worker as complicit.

**Approaches and clues:** Impressions, sand residue, readers' memory, Basto's pigment tests, cats'
black paws, account weights, decoy documents, public recitation, or legal stays offer redundant
paths.

**Failure forward:** One decisive page vanishes, but contradictions in surviving delivery weights
shift the case from document proof to procurement fraud.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** The advocate falls and the affected lawsuit survives; the coalition
above becomes a future political adversary. A copper taste at the celebration fountain offers the
unrelated Pomegranate Court problem.

### Q42 — Three Spoons Before the Flood

**Thread:** Hearth at a distant home; independent disaster and governance story.

**Hook:** Flood markers rise faster than forecast, but one neighbourhood refuses Kavi's evacuation
because its assigned refuge has been converted into a locked warehouse.

**Layers:** stubborn residents → illegal sale of refuge rights → the warehouse contains relief grain
quietly reserved for politically recognized villages only.

**Objectives:**

1. Move vulnerable people, animals, food, and records above the projected waterline.
2. Establish who controls the refuge and what safe alternatives exist now.
3. Decide how the stored grain and shelter are used during and after the flood.

**Approaches and clues:** Communal kitchens, serving spoons as boat poles and signals, roof routes,
canal captains, breaking or lawfully opening the warehouse, public inventory, and improvised rafts
all matter.

**Failure forward:** Water reaches the lower homes, but pre-positioned kitchens prevent isolation
and floating paper boats reveal which roofs still need help.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** Cleanup uncovers a paper boat asking whether an ox can predict death,
leading to the lighter but dangerous market mystery in Q43.

### Q43 — The Ox Chooses a Corpse

**Thread:** Independent social mystery.

**Hook:** At the Saffron Ox market, a valuable breeding animal kneels before three bidders in turn;
each receives an anonymous note predicting death before sunset.

**Layers:** animal prophecy → scent-triggered trained response → poisoned account tablets share the
same resin and would harm whoever moistens a stylus to calculate bids.

**Objectives:**

1. Keep the named bidders and crowded market safe without causing a stampede.
2. Determine what controls the ox's behavior and how the poison would be delivered.
3. Identify the intended victim and motive before contracts make the scheme irreversible.

**Approaches and clues:** Animal handling, scent comparison, market accounts, tablet makers, false
bidding, protective isolation, public announcement, or following the note carrier can work. The ox
also kneels at a resin-painted gate, proving behavior rather than foresight.

**Failure forward:** A bidder falls ill but survives with rapid mundane care; their incomplete sale
still reveals who needed them removed.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** The ox's new owner pays partly in rare kite cord, which carries an
impossible knot pattern and opens Q44.

### Q44 — Knots That Cross the Sky

**Thread:** Road exploration with a Deep-history aftertaste.

**Hook:** Signal kites return to Kitewife Mesa with additional knots matching a military code unused
for a century.

**Layers:** suspected spy message → cliff-creatures collect and retie bright cord → their nesting
route repeats an old shepherd evacuation warning around a newly unstable escarpment.

**Objectives:**

1. Prevent false signals from misdirecting caravans and patrols.
2. Follow the cord's route and distinguish animal behavior from human communication.
3. Interpret the historical warning and act on the present hazard it identifies.

**Approaches and clues:** Felis's kite craft, natural observation, old songs, nonlethal lures,
climbing, watching shadows, or comparing knot wear all work. Human knots tighten from both ends;
creature knots contain feathers and only one tension direction.

**Failure forward:** Officials seize the kites and mobilize against imaginary spies, but the public
movement exposes the unstable route and creates witnesses for correction.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** Evacuated travelers include a bell claimant bound for Selucia, offering
Q45 through ordinary shared transport.

### Q45 — The Bell Borrowed Twice

**Thread:** Shared history and reconciliation.

**Hook:** A mainland delegation arrives at Borrowed Bell Isle with a royal return order moments
before the bell must sound an approaching storm.

**Layers:** ownership dispute → contradictory gift and salvage accounts → the bell contains a hidden
crew list proving mutual rescue and an older promise of shared custodianship.

**Objectives:**

1. Keep the storm-warning system functioning during the dispute.
2. establish the bell's provenance through metal, inscription, songs, and family records.
3. Reach an arrangement that respects rescue memory, current safety, and lawful claims.

**Approaches and clues:** Mara's bronze knowledge, roof-tile names, Brinewife's compact, a temporary
replacement signal, shared public recitation, copying the inscription, or alternating custody are
possible. The “riddle” is a name list readable only when the bell is lit from below.

**Failure forward:** Officials remove the clapper, but islanders improvise a safe warning and the
clapper's interior reveals the decisive names.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** A post-storm wedding at the Unfinished Palace provides genuine rest
before a private-room claim begins Q46.

### Q46 — One Room Under No Roof

**Thread:** Independent legal farce with political consequences.

**Hook:** During a public wedding, a noble ropes off the Unfinished Palace's only completed room and
produces a deed granting private ownership “beneath its roof.” The palace has no roof.

**Layers:** absurd property dispute → deed language intentionally exploits unfinished architecture →
wall measurements preserve an island levy compromise that would cost the claimant's allies dearly.

**Objectives:**

1. Let the wedding and public use continue safely without prejudging the property case.
2. Test the deed, architectural history, and encoded measurements.
3. Resolve immediate access and decide how the tax discovery reaches affected islands.

**Approaches and clues:** Sola's structural expertise, performance blocking, old architect models,
measuring shadows, public custom, temporary partitions, negotiation, or a hearing in the room all
work. “Beneath its roof” may describe a painted plan rather than a built structure.

**Failure forward:** The wedding relocates to the courtyard and succeeds; the claimant occupies the
room but cannot alter it while the measurement evidence is appealed.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** Wedding bread arrives stamped like shields from Kethria, accompanied by
news that the monastery's winter flour is ruined—Q47.

## Mountains, merchants, and higher ledgers

### Q47 — The Hollow Shield Loaf

**Thread:** Hearth and winter logistics; central conspiracy optional.

**Hook:** Breadshield Monastery's ceremonial first loaf collapses hollow, revealing that damp and
weevils have spoiled much of the winter reserve.

**Layers:** storage failure → steward hid cheap damp purchases to cover charitable debt → a grain
broker knowingly sold unsafe stock while buying sound flour for a private dragon-tithe convoy.

**Objectives:**

1. Assess, sort, and stretch safe food before snow closes the pass.
2. Reconstruct the purchasing failure without delaying emergency resupply.
3. Decide how to address debt, fraud, and the suspicious convoy while protecting hungry households.

**Approaches and clues:** Tovan grades grain, shield stamps trace ovens, neighboring stores can form a
mutual-aid chain, spoiled flour has nonfood uses, caravans can be intercepted commercially or
politically, and the steward may confess if residents are fed first.

**Failure forward:** Rations tighten and some households relocate temporarily, but the loaf-check
custom reveals needs early enough to prevent famine.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** Following the convoy can enter the dragon-tithe ladder. Declining it
still closes the food crisis and sends a League customs receipt that opens Q48 independently.

### Q48 — The Warehouse That Moves at Night

**Thread:** Crown and institutional mystery with an apparent-boss ladder.

**Hook:** League inventories show relief goods entering “the Fifth Warehouse,” although only four
bonded stores exist and night watch records no carts leaving them.

**Layers:** accounting ghost → floating barge-store protected by an obsolete emergency exemption →
its master serves a customs advocate, who serves a council bloc using crisis law to corner grain
and influence the missing League-chair investigation.

**Objectives:**

1. Locate the goods and determine whether any community currently depends on them.
2. Prove how the exemption, manifests, tides, and custody chain conceal the floating store.
3. Stop present diversion while deciding whether the old emergency mechanism should survive reform.

**Approaches and clues:** Tide tables, warehouse shadows, barge drafts, duplicate seals, Venn's cargo
scents, following night pilots, creating a legitimate emergency order, or confronting buyers all
work. The apparent barge-master boss can be removed without resolving the political sponsor above.

**Failure forward:** The barge sails, but overloaded draft limits its route and public relief claims
freeze the cargo at the next port. The theft ends even if the sponsor escapes.

**Aftermath or fresh door:** The council bloc points toward a larger League succession struggle.
Meanwhile, a wildfire seed found in Pepperglass packing cloth offers a wholly unrelated ecological
expedition, preserving a fresh door outside the campaign spine.

## Additional-quest use rules

- Treat every packet as a situation, not a required scene order.
- The listed clues are examples; essential conclusions should remain reachable through at least
  three independent observations, witnesses, records, or consequences.
- Violence may occur where the setting and accepted D&D owners support it, but no quest assumes it
  is the sole or preferred answer.
- Failure changes cost, time, trust, evidence, or who must help; it does not end the campaign.
- Q31–Q48 are editorial labels only. Runtime quest IDs, states, objective updates, rewards,
  relationships, secrets, and history links require their confirmed owners.
