## Description
A root record holds a premise. A premise is not a table. Between the two sits a short chain —
world, arc, chapter, party, scene, session — and it has an order, because each link is only
checkable once the one before it exists.

## Instructions
1. Write the root through its owning contract: premise, party goals, and the tone and boundaries
   the table has agreed to. Boundaries are load-bearing — they are what a later session reads to
   know which kinds of pressure are welcome.
2. Link the campaign to its world, and to the place the story opens.
3. Give it one active arc, holding the continuing stake, and one active chapter, holding the
   present dramatic question. The chapter question should be answerable in a session or two; the
   arc stake should not be.
4. Attach every player character through the campaign's own attachment path, so that participation
   is derived rather than asserted. Exactly one active participation may exist per actor.
5. Record the current scene, pointing at a location that is furnished — see the place contract.
   Opening in an unfurnished location is the most common way a first session stalls.
6. Publish the scene's affordances if the opening needs them, remembering they are presentation
   hints and never the player's actual options.
7. Start the session last, once the chain above reads back clean. A session opened over an
   incomplete campaign records play that cannot be resumed.

## Constraints
- Playable means: root, world link, live arc, live chapter, one attached character, and an opening
  scene in a place that has been furnished.
- Conversation is not state. Anything not written and read back will not survive to next session.
