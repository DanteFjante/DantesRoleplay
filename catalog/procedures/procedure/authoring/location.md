## Description
A location row makes a place addressable. It does not make it playable. Rain, a bell tower and a
market are scenery; a toll inspector who needs an impossible number by Friday is somewhere to
play. This lists what turns the first into the second.

## Instructions
1. Write the place itself through its owning contract: kind, status, a summary that says what the
   place is *for*, and visibility. A summary that describes scenery without naming the work done
   there gives a table nothing to push against.
2. Put it somewhere. A place must terminate beneath an existing parent through containment, and
   name how a traveller arrives and leaves. An unplaced location is unreachable in play even
   though every read returns it.
3. Give it at least one person, carrying a motive through the faction-and-motive contract. Name
   what that person wants this week, not only who they are. A place with no one in it can be
   described but not talked to.
4. Give it something not yet known — a secret, a clue, or an unconfirmed rumour — through the
   knowledge contract, with its classification set. Prefer planting a clue early and revealing it
   later over inventing an explanation at the moment it is needed.
5. Give it something already known: a fact or piece of lore that explains why the place matters to
   the people who live near it. This is what lets narration stay specific when players ask a
   question nobody planned for.
6. Attach a visual through the media contract when the place is somewhere the party will return
   to, or somewhere a scene will open. Set the player and GM variants deliberately; they are
   descriptive metadata and never authorization.
7. Read every record back after writing, and confirm the place is reachable from the campaign's
   world before treating it as done.

## Constraints
- Furnished means: placed, one person who wants something, one thing not yet known, one thing
  already known, and a way in and out. Short of that, call it a draft when handing it over.
- Not every place earns an image. A corridor crossed once does not.
- Presence in a projection is not permission to say a thing aloud.
