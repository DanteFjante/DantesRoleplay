## Description
Creation gives a quest a title and three sleeping objectives. Players meet none of that. They meet
the evidence, and later they live with the consequences. Both have to exist before the quest is
offered, and the consequences have to be written down before anyone finds out which one they get.

## Instructions
1. Create the quest and its objectives through the owning creation contract, then attach it to the
   campaign's active arc and chapter as continuity context. A quest floating outside continuity
   cannot be resumed by a later session.
2. Plant the evidence before offering the quest. Each objective needs at least one clue already
   written into the world and reachable by more than one route. A quest whose only path runs
   through one clue, one roll, or one surviving person is a quest that can dead-end.
3. State what the party gets in material terms — coin, an item, standing with a faction, access to
   a place or a person — and record it where the reward is owned rather than only in narration.
4. State the experience award at creation time, so that advancement follows from what was
   attempted rather than from what a later session decides it should have been worth.
5. Write the failure outcome as deliberately as the success outcome. Failure should move the
   situation on — the carriers get further, the toll goes up, someone else pays — rather than
   leaving the table where it was. Record both as outcomes the lifecycle contract can transition to.
6. Name who profits and who is hurt, and connect those to existing motives. A quest that no
   recorded person wants either way will read as an errand.
7. On resolution, record the transition through the lifecycle contract and read the quest back.
   Narration never advances a quest by describing it as advanced.

## Constraints
- Ready to offer means: continuity attached, more than one route to each objective, a material
  reward, an experience award, and both endings written.
- A finished adventure stays finished. A later revelation may reframe why it mattered; it may not
  retract what it was worth.
- Never reveal an unrevealed clue to rescue a stalled table. Add another route instead.
