## Description

Resolves one prepared D&D journey from an authored generic route profile through atomic arrival.

## Instructions

Use `dnd2024.mechanic.travel.execute` when the route, endpoints, traveller, world, pace, and current
route and clock revisions are known. The route must already carry an active generic route,
availability, and `dnd2024.exploration.route-profile`.

Supply one closed `travel` object with new journey and exposure-schedule entity IDs, `walk`, the
selected `slow`, `normal`, or `fast` pace, and the observed route revision, route fingerprint, and
clock revision. Bind the traveller, origin, destination, route, and world roles exactly.

If the route profile declares `navigation.required: true`, also bind `navigator` and copy the
profile's ability, skill, DC, and roll circumstances into `navigationCheck`. Omit both the role and
input when navigation is not required. The host invokes the pinned ability-check child only in the
required case; the caller never supplies a roll result.

The mechanic rejects closed routes, impossible modes or paces, mismatched endpoints, stale route
or clock evidence, unsupported distance units, and failed navigation. Success atomically creates a
completed journey, creates any pending exposure schedule, moves the traveller, advances the world
clock, and records typed journey, arrival, and clock events. Reuse the same operation id to replay;
do not invent new IDs or retry with a new idempotency key after an uncertain response.

## Constraints

- Distance, terrain, visibility, navigation, and exposure policy come only from the active route profile.
- The AI never supplies a navigation result; the pinned ability-check mechanic rolls it.
- Pending exposure intervals do not resolve their hazard before the focused hazard mechanic exists.
- A failed child or failed transaction commits no movement, journey, exposure, arrival, or clock change.
