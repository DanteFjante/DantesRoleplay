Lanternback Colossus

Story seeds

A wealthy family offers to restore the cracked left hand if the communal hearth becomes its private memorial. A lantern bearing the name of a living traveller appears in a row usually used for the dead. Tessa needs someone to recover her first repair sketch from Highmead; without it, a well-meant new brace may obstruct the spring.

What lies behind them

The strange lantern was hung by a frightened descendant using an old family name, not by a power predicting death. Beneath the spring is a reused grain chamber containing evidence that several respectable households charged for shelter during a later famine. The chamber is structurally unsafe and has no registered open approach. The stone figure itself neither wakes nor grants wishes.

Crownroot Cathedral

Story seeds

The old teacher returns and finds the pupil she remembers as a boy preparing to retire. A lucrative new fruit variety threatens the unharvested bird strip. Two villages each possess a sound but incompatible record of a donated branch, and both need its crop to fund their winter kitchen.

What lies behind them

One celebrated branch was quietly replaced after disease killed it. Its fruit is excellent, but a merchant markets it as the unchanged scion of the original compact. Exposing the claim threatens honest gardeners as well as the merchant. The Guest's limited capacity remains limited; the tree cannot be used to extend protection across all Alderwick by selling cuttings.

Skyharp of Roven

Story seeds

The harp sounds a retired warning while the official forecast promises clear passage. A collector offers enough money for winter supplies in exchange for the oldest resonator. A griffin is scouting the old slope again, and both convoy owners and shepherds want the keeper to call it somebody else's problem.

What lies behind them

A repair using a different bronze alloy has shifted one resonator's note. That explains the false warning, but not a newly uncovered winch mark belonging to an unrecorded salvage crew. The crew's cache is on the near side of the gorge. Neither it nor the harp creates a bypass around the Unfinished Stair.

The Upward Fall

Story seeds

Water continues to rise while the downstream gauge falls: a hidden leak or diversion is robbing someone of a promised share. A theatrical mage claims to have invented the ancient lift. The replacement mineral bed is trapped in an ownership dispute, and shutting the fall for repair will cost one village its early vegetable crop.

What lies behind them

A recently altered garden drain, approved under an obsolete property name, accounts for most of the missing water. Nobody involved understood the whole plan. A small remaining discrepancy points to a sealed inspection pocket containing the original rejected channel proposal. The fall cannot lift travellers or provide unlimited power; attempts to enlarge the flow damage its carefully prepared bed.

The Hanging Harbour

Story seeds

A valuable keel has been pledged to two creditors who each claim the next repaired ship. A replacement chain arrives with excellent paperwork and the wrong iron. An apprentice finds a refuge account inside a reused hull, complicating a celebrated story about which merchants kept people fed during the sailing shortage.

What lies behind them

The chain was substituted by a supplier trying to keep an underpaid workshop solvent, not by a foreign saboteur. The material is unsafe for the heaviest cradle. Records hidden in a patched rib also show that the cooperative once accepted a profitable priority repair that delayed a poorer community's boat. The lower loading gear can be shut without cutting off the inspected upper-yard path.

Emberglass Whale

Story seeds

A collector produces a fragment that appears to complete a pattern absent from every published drawing. A new copper claim would require a cart road beneath the tail. Someone sells accurate copies as relics, but the workshop making them needs the income to repair the genuine monument.

What lies behind them

The whale's hollow ribs once demonstrated differences in furnace temperatures and sound, combining art with a teaching instrument. Its joined construction was obscured by later glazing. The missing pattern is an old repair key rather than a prophecy. Karsh did not make the whale, and its existence neither proves the old tithe was legitimate nor cancels real obligations arising elsewhere.

Rain Crown of Orovant

Story seeds

A new translation turns an old right to draw water into a claim to own the cistern. The crown's veils gather much less mist after a profitable kiln begins work upwind. An elderly veil-weaver can repair the oldest knotting pattern, but her estranged apprentice owns the only suitable mesh.

What lies behind them

Soot from the kiln has fouled the veils, while an unnoticed storage leak accounts for a second portion of the loss. Both problems can be repaired without blaming the crown's magic. One original tablet records a smaller, excluded carrying community whose claim was never translated into the dominant trade language. The floating stones do not offer a controllable means of flight.

Glass-Tide Palace

Story seeds

A conservator needs a lost formula for sea-glass mortar while salvage bidders prepare to dismantle the lower arcade. A seal from a disputed cargo lies beneath a transparent floor panel. Fishers oppose a plan to drain the building because its lower rooms now shelter the young fish that support their catch.

What lies behind them

The suspicious cargo seal reached the palace in a later repair shipment, so its presence cannot prove the original cargo landed here. A surviving clerk's index identifies the shipment, but it is mixed with an heirloom household archive in Istravel. Draining the whole structure would damage both its foundations and the nursery habitat; preserving the upper hall remains possible.

The Orchardback

Story seeds

An old agreement promises fruit to a household that has split into three distant branches. The tortoise begins waking during harvest, leaving growers to choose between valuable fruit and a safe departure. A customs official argues that a travelling orchard should pay import duty every time it swims back into the bay.

What lies behind them

The animal responds to water temperature and feeding opportunities, not to a prophecy about the campaign. A recent deeper mooring stake is irritating a resting flipper and should be moved before the harvest dispute is resolved. The oldest donor book includes a graft from a household erased from later registers, creating a modest evidentiary link to living people rather than proof of an ancient royal heir. No party can command it as a conveyance.
