# World Creation Ledger

**Status:** Active canon ledger — in progress  
**Purpose:** Portable record of the world being created through the Luna Worldsmith interview.  
**Rules foundation:** D&D 5e 2024, using existing rules for now.  
**Canon policy:** Confirmed entries are current canon. Proposed entries require approval. Open entries are intentionally unresolved.

**World name:** Thalorien  
**Live server state:** Active world root saved as `world.thalorien`; the Brackenford campaign namespace is `campaign.thalorien.brackenford`.

## Confirmed canon

### Scope

- The setting begins with a broad world premise that can support a local starting setting.
- The world contains **seven kingdoms**.
- The world is named **Thalorien**.
- The kingdoms have medieval-sounding names and confirmed founding domains; their later distinctive cultures and current political personalities remain open for development.

### Era and atmosphere

- The world is in a long, peaceful era.
- Enough time has passed that most people are accustomed to peace.
- Most people know the previous bad age through stories told by ancestors, rather than personal experience.
- The setting is warm high fantasy with travel, exploration, quiet melancholy, ancient history, and occasional focused danger.
- Peace is a central value of the setting.
- The world has a rich, layered history made up of multiple eras, obscure cultures, secrets, and ancient structures whose purposes most people no longer understand.

### Core promise

> In a peaceful age after legendary heroes have passed into history, ordinary travelers journey between seven kingdoms, discovering that even small places, brief friendships, and forgotten stories can shape the future.

### Foundational truth

> Every age has left something behind, and the modern kingdoms live among remnants they do not fully understand.

## Confirmed desired play

- Exploration is the primary activity, with beautiful locations, scenic discovery, and poetic or prose-forward narration suitable for listening.
- The world should support generated visual scenes when useful.
- Adventures should include hidden quests, secrets, challenging clues, and occasional riddles.
- Thrilling episodes should appear at important moments, including frightening revelations and situations where a companion's life may be at stake.
- Relationships with NPCs should grow over time and reflect the party's actions.
- NPCs should sometimes recount the party's actions from their own perspectives, preserving different interpretations and memories of what happened.

## Confirmed cosmology

- Thalorien uses the standard D&D gods, planes, and divine order as its underlying reality.
- Local churches and peoples worship and interpret those gods through their own traditions. Specific local practices can be developed later without changing the standard D&D cosmology.
- Local peoples and ancient cultures preserve different creation myths and beliefs about death.
- Contradictory ancient evidence may make the true history of the world difficult to reconstruct.

## Confirmed pre-Thalmos history

- Before the founding of the Thalmos Empire, a massive outbreak of monsters appeared from seemingly everywhere.
- Thalmon travelled the world and slew the monsters, discovering that they emerged from caves beneath the ground.
- Attempts to seal the smaller caves caused new caves to appear, while sealing the largest caves did not produce new ones.
- The largest caves were sealed, protective measures were organized around the smaller caves, and a systematic defence against the monsters was established. This response became the foundation of the Thalmos Empire.
- The intelligent races were brought to the brink of extinction, with humanity suffering the worst losses.
- Settlements around the future empire already existed during the crisis. They were granted amnesty in return for helping save humanity, and later kingdoms continued to respect that protection.
- Settlements inhabited by species other than humans are older than recorded history and did not form with the Thalmos Empire. Some survived the monster invasion better than later civilizations and became important keepers of guarded historical memory.
- Elven society has the longest lifespan among the surviving peoples and guards historical memory better than any other culture.
- Dwarves live mostly in the mountains and were better equipped to protect themselves and their history from the monster invasion.

## Historical labels

- **The Veiled Age** is the modern scholarly name for the final pre-Thalmos age, when the monster invasion destroyed much of the world's history and the settlements that preserved the most knowledge began guarding their records closely.
- The Veiled Age is a later descriptive label, not necessarily the age's original name. The true names of the Veiled Age and earlier ages may remain lost or survive only in protected oral traditions, sealed archives, and fragmentary ruins.
- **The Sudden Calamity** is the name given to the great monster invasion.

### Confirmed present-day historical context

- The great Thalos war ended more than **1,530 years ago**, placing its losses and lessons beyond the living memory of nearly everyone.
- Over the centuries, the nations that lost so much in the war replaced its old points of interest with newer ones. The seven nations are now more balanced than they were when the war began.
- The current post-Thalmos age is the best-documented period of Thalorien's history. Knowledge of the world before the kingdoms and the Thalmos Empire survives only in fragments because the Sudden Calamity destroyed so much, and the oldest records are closely guarded.

### Confirmed keepers of older history

- **Elaris** is the great Elven settlement around the World Tree, the largest Elven settlement in Thalorien.
- Elaris's historians are known as the **Keepers of the Long Remembrance**. They preserve living recollections and guarded records reaching farther back than any human kingdom's history.
- **Kharad Veyr** is a venerable Dwarven mountain settlement whose archivists maintain the **Deep Ledger**, recording craft, lineage, treaties, and remembered dangers in stone and metal.
- The Deep Ledger preserves fragments of history that survived the monster invasion. The Elven and Dwarven records are valuable, but neither culture possesses a complete account of the distant past.

### Confirmed ancient civilization

- The **Nethravai** were the civilization that created the oldest known dungeons, dating from roughly **4,000 to 6,000 years ago**.
- They built maze-like complexes to contain monsters from the deep caves and later developed monster wards that kept those creatures from reaching the overworld.
- The Nethravai's own history, name, and culture remain only partly understood. Their dungeons and surviving works are better known than the people who made them.

## Confirmed Thalmos succession and imperial legacy

- Thalmon was widely beloved during his reign and used his unusual freedom to build what he regarded as a utopian project of peace and civic prosperity. Its shortcomings, excluded groups, and unintended consequences remain open rather than assumed away.
- Thalmon had eight children—six sons and two daughters—each educated in a different governing discipline. After his death, they attempted to rule together through an eight-sibling council.
- Their complementary educations instead produced incompatible priorities. Public frustration became an organized rebellion; Prince Oberon was killed during the unrest, and the seven survivors acted together to end it.
- The seven surviving siblings then divided the Thalmos Empire into seven countries as fairly as they could, so each possessed a major source of value and authority.
- Four siblings received kingdoms containing magic towers; Aldron received the central inland kingdom and the University; the remaining two received kingdoms centred on the empire's two major trade hubs.
- The seven rulers still convene annually in the eldest sibling's kingdom. They may advise one another, but each ruler retains final authority in their own kingdom.
- During the current age's roughly fourth century, unequal power and weakening respect for the constitution led to the Great Seven-Kingdom War.

## Confirmed institutions, magic, and cultural legacy

- Thalmon's constitution constrained future rulers. Four independent constitutional magic towers could challenge or veto decisions that violated it, and trained battle magicians able to oppose an unlawful ruler.
- Three towers remain active today; one fell during the great war. Its battle magicians scattered, were hunted, and often survived under concealed identities. Their secret schools and research networks later helped give rise to the Dark Market and the wider criminal underworld.
- Thalmon founded a University that was initially open to everyone. It educated civil servants and commanders, while the associated Military School taught practical martial skill. Separate schools of business, arts, and non-magical engineering later competed with the broad University through demonstrations and contests.
- During the great war, the School of the Arts was destroyed; its teachers and students became travelling troupes that now carry performance, news, and culture between kingdoms.
- Thalmon's empire also maintained welfare, though its exact programs and what survives today remain unknown. Trade roads connected major and provincial hubs to channel commerce across the empire.
- Magic is known but uncommon in ordinary life. Deliberate spellcasting usually needs expensive education, while rune-enchanted practical items—such as a wealthy inn's freezer box—are the magical tools most people encounter.
- Churches remain institutionally distinct from university-trained arcane magic. Clerics often form healing networks.
- Alongside standard D&D magic, Thalorien has local magical traditions known as **knot magic** and **magical music**. Their methods, limits, cultural origins, and relationship to university magic remain open questions for later exploration.
- **Codex decision rule:** Thalorien remains materially medieval. Magic supplements ordinary labor, travel, institutions, and crafts rather than replacing them at scale. Rune tools are occasional, costly, specialized, and maintained like valuable tools; they are not everyday industrial technology.
- The seven kingdoms developed distinct cultures, including their own customs for lineage and succession. Their relationships range from close ties to political, economic, territorial, and historical tensions, but none is currently preparing for war.

## Confirmed wider-world context

- Thalos is the limit of the known world, not necessarily the limit of Thalorien. Lands beyond the continent and its largely uncharted oceans are unknown rather than known not to exist.
- Monsters sometimes wash ashore from the sea, suggesting unknown ecosystems or places beyond the horizon. Creatures of legend also exist, though most knowledge of them is folklore, disputed sightings, and old stories.
- Major settlements and routes are safer than the wilderness, where royal protection weakens and bandits, monsters, dangerous creatures, and other hazards remain meaningful.
- Bandits, monsters, and occasional corrupt officials still threaten the long peace. Some scholars think the current age may be nearing its end, but this is interpretation rather than established fact.

## Confirmed ancient dungeons and wards

- The ancient defensive dungeons were created during an era that flourished roughly 4,000–6,000 years ago. There is not enough research to establish that any surviving dungeon is older than that range. The builders were aware of the monsters beneath the ground.
- The dungeons used maze-like structures to confuse monsters trying to escape into the overworld, with traps and supply chests for intelligent peoples who descended to eliminate excess monsters.
- Monster wards capable of preventing creatures from entering the overworld were discovered about 4,000 years ago. The wards were placed on the dungeons, after which the dungeons were forgotten because they were no longer needed. No such wards remain active today.
- After the wards were placed and the dungeons were forgotten, the monsters inside grew stronger and more numerous over at least 4,000 years while the wards gradually weakened.
- The ancient wards eventually waned and failed, causing the great monster invasion. The invasion was especially damaging to humanity because monster populations had grown while human defensive capabilities had atrophied during the long period of safety.

## Confirmed postwar peace

- After the great Thalos war, the kingdoms had lost so much that they were humbled and became willing to listen to the settlements that urged them to return to Thalmon's founding ideals.
- The settlements, especially the elves, reminded the kingdoms of Thalmon's dream, his wisdom, and the old constitution that had been meant to guard peace.
- The kingdoms established additional constitutional and political guardrails intended to prevent another war from arising among them.
- A shared **Book of Truth** was created to teach every citizen their rights and responsibilities and to preserve stories of history and the dangers of war.
- The kingdoms made sustained efforts to teach their citizens to reject waging war, seeking a broad civic agreement that war must never again be chosen between the kingdoms.
- Churches, clerics, and priests were entrusted with spreading the Book of Truth as a civic peace duty separate from their religious responsibilities. In return, the kingdoms granted the churches greater authority and freedom.

## Confirmed quiet threat to the peace

- About 150 years ago, merchants in Thalorien's main trading hub grew strong and bold enough to form a secret coalition.
- The coalition began by exchanging market advice and industry knowledge, but that cooperation quickly evolved into manipulating the market as a whole.
- After learning to influence vendors and markets, the merchants sought to influence consumers as well, so people would accept worse deals without recognizing how they were being manipulated.
- The Book of Truth stood in the way of this consumer manipulation because its history, warnings about war, and teaching about rights and responsibilities encouraged citizens to question unfair arrangements.
- The merchants plan to influence the kingdoms by arguing that roughly 1,500 years have passed since the Book of Truth was released, that the danger of war is gone, and that its peace education has become harmful indoctrination that prevents free minds, progress, and new ideas.
- As the story begins, the leaders of the seven kingdoms are interested in what the merchants are proposing.
- The quiet threat to the current peace is the coalition's effort to weaken the shared civic education that protects citizens from manipulation, while persuading the kingdoms that this safeguard is obsolete.

## Confirmed economy and currency

- For roughly the first 1,500 years after the Thalmos Empire was divided, the seven kingdoms used the same currency.
- They later adopted separate local currencies after realizing that Evandos, in the north, had access to metals from its Northern Cold Highlands that were scarce elsewhere.
- Rather than let the northern kingdom control the common currency or pay it for access to those metals, the kingdoms chose distinct local metal compositions for their currencies.
- Even after Evandos lost the trade hub of Harrowfall during the great war, it could rely on coin production from the northern mountains until roughly 500 years ago.

### Confirmed GM-only faction state

- **The Gilded Concord** is the secret Merrowgate-centered merchant coalition. Its members include powerful merchant houses, factors, lenders, warehouse owners, and shipping agents rather than a public guild.
- Ordinary business ties, market advice, and commercial competition make its members appear unremarkable. The Concord's coordinated market manipulation and political purpose are GM-only secrets.
- The Concord has no single headquarters. Its Merrowgate network operates through counting houses, private dining rooms, ships' offices, warehouses, credit arrangements, and exchanged favors.
- Its ready agenda is to win broad royal support for reducing or ending Book of Truth education before organized opposition recognizes the coordinated effort.
- The Concord has privately placed its argument before all seven rulers. They are listening, but no named ruler has openly allied with it.
- **Server records:** `faction.thalorien.gilded-concord` and the GM-only secrets `secret.thalorien.gilded-concord-conspiracy`, `secret.thalorien.gilded-concord-merrowgate-network`, and `secret.thalorien.gilded-concord-ruler-pressure`.

## Confirmed locations

- **Aldros:** The central and only landlocked kingdom of Thalorien. It was founded by Aldron, the first prince, whose domain was diplomacy and administration. Its central position makes it a natural meeting ground among the kingdoms.
- **Server record:** `location.thalorien.aldros` — live as a draft `region` directly contained by `world.thalorien`.
- **The World Tree Grounds:** Neutral shared territory at the absolute center of the continent. It is politically independent of Aldros while directly adjoining it.
- **The World Tree:** A sacred tree approximately 2,000 meters high and 50 meters wide, nested within the World Tree Grounds.
- **World Tree Elven Enclave:** The largest Elven settlement in Thalorien, built around the sacred tree.
- **Server records:** `location.thalorien.world-tree-grounds`, `location.thalorien.world-tree`, and `location.thalorien.world-tree-elven-enclave`.

### Added kingdom and volcanic locations

- Six kingdoms form a hexagonal ring around central Aldros: **Minevros** to the northwest, **Evandos** to the north, **Rhiannos** to the northeast, **Valeros** to the southeast, **Merceros** to the south, and **Waylos** to the southwest. Minevros and Valeros oppose one another across the central kingdom.
- Draft region records now exist for **Evandos**, **Minevros**, **Valeros**, **Rhiannos**, **Merceros**, and **Waylos**, using this arrangement and their established founding domains without exact coordinates or routes.
- Each outer kingdom directly borders Aldros and its two neighboring outer kingdoms; these thirteen canonical adjacencies, including Aldros's direct adjacency to the World Tree Grounds, are stored on the live server.
- A **Southwestern Volcanic Region** now exists as a draft region within **Waylos**, containing a southwestern volcano and an outer **Volcanic Island Chain**.
- **Minevros, Valeros, Rhiannos, and Waylos** each had a magic tower. The Valeros tower was destroyed during the great Thalos war.
- Elven and dwarven historical-keeper facts, and the name **The Sudden Calamity**, are now synchronized to the MCP server.
- The corrected dungeon timeline is now synchronized to `fact.thalorien.ancient-dungeons`.

### Geography around the World Tree Grounds

- **Crownmere** is the courtly capital of Aldros, east of the World Tree Grounds. Its embassies and administrative halls make it the kingdom's natural civic seat.
- **Larkspire University** is Thalmon's historic university west of the World Tree Elven Enclave in Aldros. It remains a center of civic, scholarly, magical, and martial education.
- Merceros lies south of the World Tree Grounds, while Evandos lies to the north.
- **Merrowgate** is the surviving southern trade city of Merceros, known for its quays, warehouses, tollhouses, and merchant houses.
- **Harrowfall** is the fallen northern trade city of Evandos, beside the distinct Northern Cold Highlands.
- Valeros contains the continent's southeastern mountain range. Harrowfall's former trade advantage came instead from a distinct northern cold-highland range.

**Server records:** `location.thalorien.crownmere`, `location.thalorien.larkspire-university`, `location.thalorien.merrowgate`, and `location.thalorien.harrowfall` are live draft locations under Aldros, Aldros, Merceros, and Evandos respectively.

### Confirmed history of Harrowfall

- During the great Thalos war, Merceros joined with its neighbor Valeros and destroyed Harrowfall.
- Before its destruction, Harrowfall had become much more prosperous than Merrowgate. This embarrassed Merceros, a commerce-oriented kingdom, while Evandos was known for being founded on law and order.
- Harrowfall's access to the distinct northern cold highlands allowed it to freeze and preserve much more of its supplies for sale during the off-season, giving it a major commercial advantage over Merrowgate. These highlands are not Valeros's southeastern mountain range.
- The king of Merceros could not tolerate Harrowfall's prosperity surpassing Merrowgate. He decided there should be only one major trade hub and ordered the attack to eliminate his commercial rival.
- Merceros's ruler, driven by greed, ordered the troops to slaughter every living being in Harrowfall after the city had been defeated.
- The resentment, despair, and sorrow left lingering in Harrowfall welcomed evil spirits. The spirits feasted on the dead and settled in the ruined city.
- No one can enter Harrowfall now. The ruined trade city remains occupied and guarded by the evil spirits that settled there after the massacre.

### Confirmed regional features and magic towers

- **The Greenmantle** is Valeros's great western forest. Its settled edge is useful, green, and quietly beautiful, while goblins and stranger dangers haunt the deeper interior. Valeros patrols that frontier, and the other kingdoms compensate it for bearing the burden.
- **The Oathshield Range** is Valeros's southeastern mountain range, named for the kingdom's defensive founding purpose. Its high passes and old watch paths make it a natural place for both protection and isolation.
- **The Starwell Tower** is Minevros's active magic tower, devoted to scholarship, observation, and the keeping of long records.
- **The Dawnbell Tower** is Rhiannos's active magic tower, associated with healing, refuge, and the careful joining of divine and arcane learning.
- **The Emberwright Tower** is Waylos's active magic tower near its southwestern volcanic region, dedicated to engineering, craft, and making useful things safely.
- **The Bannerfall Tower** is Valeros's destroyed magic tower. Its forbidden, dangerous ruins lie in the Oathshield Range and remain reachable, though they are not safely open.
- **Mount Cinderwake** is the great volcano of Waylos's Southwestern Volcanic Region.
- **The Ashen Crown Isles** are Waylos's offshore volcanic island chain beyond Mount Cinderwake, with black beaches and fertile slopes.
- The broader southwestern region also includes a broad desert shaped by the volcano's heat, ash, and rain shadow.
- The southeastern continent is marked by high tropical cliffs and deep tropical jungle valleys, with many difficult-to-reach places between the heights and green depths.
- Farther north in the same broad southeastern region, the terrain becomes Mediterranean-like, with forests, agricultural fields, villages, and the capital.
- Toward the northeastern kingdom, the land grows more heavily forested; in the kingdom's far north, the forests give way to snow and cold mountain country.

## Proposed canon

These ideas have been suggested but are not final until approved:

- The world’s main themes are peace versus complacency, remembering the past without being ruled by it, change versus permanence, and connection across generations.
- The seven kingdoms will have distinct identities and create room for alliances, rivalries, trade, and border tensions.
- **Starting region:** The settled edge of the Greenmantle, in Valeros, is the confirmed local starting region.

### Confirmed local starting setting — Brackenford

- **Brackenford** is a Valeros frontier village on the eastern settled edge of the Greenmantle, where an old stone ford carries the road between farm country and the forest patrols. It is large enough for an inn, shrine, smith, small market, and a few visiting monster-hunters, but small enough that people notice an unfamiliar face and remember a kindness.
- Its everyday character is cozy and practical: morning bread, wet cloaks by the inn hearth, children collecting bright leaves for autumn wreaths, woodcutters sharing forest gossip, and patrols returning at dusk to appreciative neighbors. Most families can get by, but nobody forgets that the woods are close.
- Brackenford matters beyond itself because Valeros's patrols, monster-hunting companies, timber trade, and frontier supplies all pass through it. A problem there can become a test of Valeros's role in keeping the wider peace safe from the Greenmantle's dangers.
- **GM-only first disturbance:** Goblin attacks have become less frequent, not more. Tracks at the forest edge show whole bands moving deeper in with supplies rather than raiding outward, while birds vanish from the old patrol road at twilight.
- **GM-only hidden root:** A collapsed waystone cellar beneath an abandoned charcoal-burner's hut may conceal an old descent into an ancient defensive dungeon. The goblins may not be fleeing a new threat—they may be gathering around something that has begun to wake.
- **Story promise:** Brackenford can begin with warmth, local favors, and relationships, then gradually reveal a larger mystery through contradictory gossip, old stones, missing patrol signs, and the perspectives of the people who call the village home.

The live MCP database contains the Brackenford location, its public location fact, the two GM-only mysteries, and the two recurring NPCs below. It intentionally contains no Brackenford quest, dungeon, rumour, or clue record yet.

## Confirmed player character — Orban

- **Orban** is 24, born in autumn, 190 cm tall and slender. He grew up in a traveling troupe that raised children communally rather than through strict individual parenting.
- His closest friend was **Nara**, a performing bear. After the troupe became lost and starving in the Waylos desert, they ate their animals, including Nara. Orban could not forgive them and left alone.
- Orban is a charming musician and performer with circus-trained nimbleness, sleight of hand, mundane illusion tricks, thrown-knife skill, and improvised-combat ability. He currently carries no weapon.
- His possessions include a magical inherited ocarina passed through the troupe's rules of musical-instrument inheritance and a durable dark cloak that helps him become inconspicuous.
- He is nearly cursed at starting fires and is afraid of heights.
- Live database actor: `actor.thalorien.brackenford.orban`; campaign participation is active under `campaign.thalorien.brackenford`.
- Codex-authored provisional details: the Bard class direction and the working cloak name **The Penumbra Mantle**. Neither is a permanent mechanical ruling.

## Codex-authored settlement additions

The following additions were authored by Codex at the user's request on 2026-08-22. They are intentionally signed as assistant-authored material so they remain distinguishable from user-confirmed canon and can be revised later:

- **Settlement Patterns of the Peaceful Era:** Modern settlements usually grow around dependable water, maintained roads, productive land, and a shared public heart such as a market square, shrine, hall, or inn. They are practical and welcoming rather than heavily militarized.
- **The Hearthside Custom:** Hospitality is widely respected. Inns, shrines, village halls, and generous households offer warmth, news, and a place to sleep, with travelers expected to contribute when they can.
- **Frontier Watch Practices:** Settlements near forests, mountains, and monster-haunted reaches share responsibility through patrols, watch posts, warning bells, and trained monster-hunters while preserving ordinary life.
